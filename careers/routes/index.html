Routes after school: apprenticeships, college and university

There's more than one way into design, engineering and construction careers. Many people move between routes: an HND can take you into year 2 or 3 of a degree, and an apprenticeship can lead to a degree.

S5 or S6, alongside your Highers

Foundation Apprenticeship

Spend part of the week at college and with an employer while still at school. Engineering, Civil Engineering and Creative & Digital Media are common choices.

Level: SCQF 6: the same level as a Higher

Good because

  • Real work experience on your CV
  • Many universities accept it in place of a non-essential Higher (e.g. Strathclyde)
  • Helps you decide if a career suits you

Watch out for

  • Takes up option-column time
  • Check with your school which FAs it offers

Leave school at 16 or later

Modern Apprenticeship

A real job: you're employed, paid at least the apprentice minimum wage, and work towards a qualification. There are over 100 frameworks, including Engineering, Construction (Carpentry & Joinery) and Fabrication.

Level: SCQF 5 to 11, depending on the framework

Good because

  • Earn while you learn
  • No student debt
  • Most take 1 to 4 years

Watch out for

  • You need to find and apply for a vacancy
  • Competition for the best employers is high: apply early in S5/S6

After S5 or S6

College: HNC and HND

Practical, career-focused courses. An HND can lead straight into year 2 or 3 of a degree. For example, Edinburgh College's Mechanical Engineering University Route is HNC then HND at college, then years 3 and 4 at Heriot-Watt.

Level: HNC is SCQF 7 (1 year); HND is SCQF 8 (2 years)

Good because

  • Lower entry requirements than university (often 1–2 Highers)
  • Smaller classes and lots of hands-on work
  • Can still finish with an honours degree in 4 years

Watch out for

  • Check which university and year the HND links to before you start
  • Some routes need a B grade in the HND

After S6 (you must have left school before you start)

Graduate Apprenticeship

A full honours degree while you're employed. You work most of the week and study at university, often one day a week. Frameworks include Engineering: Design and Manufacture, Civil Engineering, and Construction and the Built Environment.

Level: SCQF 9 to 11: the same degree as full-time students get

Good because

  • A degree with no tuition fees and a salary
  • 4 years of work experience by the time you graduate
  • No maximum age

Watch out for

  • You must be employed in a GA job: search vacancies from January of S6
  • Fewer places than full-time degrees

After S5 or S6, applying through UCAS

University degree

Scottish honours degrees usually take 4 years (MEng/MArch 5 or more). Eligible Scottish students don't pay tuition fees: apply to SAAS for funding each year.

Level: SCQF 9 to 11: Ordinary, Honours or integrated Master's

Good because

  • Widest choice of careers, including chartered engineer and architect
  • Strong Advanced Highers can mean starting in year 2
  • Widening access offers can be lower than standard offers

Watch out for

  • Check required subjects as well as grades
  • Art schools need a portfolio, and some courses interview
