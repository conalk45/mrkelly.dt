Transistor as a switch

An NPN transistor has a base, collector and emitter. When the voltage at the base is about 0.7 V or more, the transistor switches on and a larger current flows from collector to emitter, operating the output (a relay, LED, buzzer or motor). A small input current controls a larger output current.

  • A base resistor limits the base current to protect the transistor.
  • A relay lets a low-current control circuit switch a separate, higher-current or higher-voltage output circuit.
  • A protection diode across the relay coil protects the transistor from the high reverse voltage produced when the coil switches off.

What catches people out

  • Didn't name a transistor as the component that switches on a lamp from a microcontroller or sensor. A transistor is needed to switch a high-current output. N5 2025

Key terms

Transistor switch-on voltageAbout 0.7 V at the base.
Base resistorLimits base current to protect the transistor.
RelayLets a low-current circuit switch a separate high-current circuit.
Protection diodeAcross the relay coil; protects the transistor from reverse voltage when the coil switches off.

2. Why is a diode placed across a relay coil?

  1. To light up when the relay is on
  2. To increase the current through the coil
  3. To protect the transistor from reverse voltage
  4. To switch the relay on and off faster
Show answer
C: To protect the transistor from reverse voltage. Back-EMF protection.
