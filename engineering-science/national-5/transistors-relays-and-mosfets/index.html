Transistor as a switch

An NPN transistor has a base, collector and emitter. When the voltage at the base is about 0.7 V or more, the transistor switches on and a larger current flows from collector to emitter, operating the output (a relay, LED, buzzer or motor). A small input current controls a larger output current.

  • A base resistor limits the base current to protect the transistor.
  • A relay lets a low-current control circuit switch a separate, higher-current or higher-voltage output circuit.
  • A protection diode across the relay coil protects the transistor from the high reverse voltage produced when the coil switches off.

Key terms

Transistor switch-on voltage
About 0.7 V at the base.
Base resistor
Limits base current to protect the transistor.
Relay
Lets a low-current circuit switch a separate high-current circuit.
Protection diode
Across the relay coil; protects the transistor from reverse voltage when the coil switches off.

Quick check

1. An NPN transistor switches on at a base voltage of about…

  1. 0.07 V
  2. 0.7 V
  3. 7 V
  4. 70 V
Show answer
B: 0.7 V. About 0.7 V.

2. Why is a diode placed across a relay coil?

  1. To light up
  2. To protect the transistor from reverse voltage
  3. To increase current
  4. To switch the relay
Show answer
B: To protect the transistor from reverse voltage. Back-EMF protection.

3. Which component lets a 5 V circuit switch a 230 V heater?

  1. LDR
  2. Relay
  3. Diode
  4. Buzzer
Show answer
B: Relay. Relay.
