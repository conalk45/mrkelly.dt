Conservation of energy

Energy can't be created or destroyed, only changed from one form to another. In real systems some energy is always "lost" as unwanted heat and sound, often through friction. An energy audit lists the energy in, the useful energy out and the losses.

Forms and sources of energy

Forms include kinetic, potential, heat (thermal), electrical, chemical, light, sound, nuclear and magnetic.

  • Fossil fuels: coal, oil and gas: burned, so they release CO₂ and will run out.
  • Nuclear: fission of uranium produces heat, which makes steam to turn a turbine and generator.
  • Renewable: wind, wave, tidal, hydro-electric and solar.

Energy is the capacity to do work, measured in joules (J). Power is how fast energy is transferred, measured in watts (W = J/s). Think of energy as the fuel in the tank and power as how quickly it's used.

An energy transfer moves energy (electricity along wires); a transformation changes its form (chemical → kinetic). Every transformation wastes some energy, usually as heat and sound.

Calculation technique

  1. Write the formula.
  2. Convert units first: hours to seconds (7.5 h = 27 000 s), kJ to J, mm to m where needed.
  3. Substitute, then rearrange (transpose).
  4. Give the answer to 2 significant figures with a unit.
  5. Don't round in the middle of a calculation; round only the final answer.

Each step usually carries a mark: substitution, transposition and answer with unit. Follow-on marks mean a wrong earlier value doesn't stop you being awarded the later marks.

Worked examples: energy changing form

Potential to kinetic. A 50 kg object falls freely from 30 m. Ep = mgh = 50 × 9.8 × 30 = 14 700 J. All of it becomes kinetic energy at impact: 14 700 = ½ × 50 × v², so v² = 588 and v = 24 m/s.

Electrical to heat. A kettle uses 24 kJ to heat 2 kg of water to 100 °C (no losses). 24 000 = 4180 × 2 × ΔT, so ΔT = 2.87 °C and the water started at 97 °C.

Efficiency in three steps. A coffee machine (230 V, 22 A) heats 0.15 kg of water from 20 °C to 85 °C in 15 s.

  1. Energy in: Ee = VIt = 230 × 22 × 15 = 75 900 J
  2. Useful energy out: Eh = cmΔT = 4180 × 0.15 × 65 = 40 755 J
  3. Efficiency: η = 40 755 ÷ 75 900 × 100 = 54%

The other 46% (35.1 kJ) is wasted, as heat to the casing and air, and sound. Efficiency can never be more than 100%.

Energy audits

An energy audit lists all the energy going into and coming out of a system, so you can see where energy is wasted. Total energy in = useful energy out + wasted energy. Show it as a diagram: the input on the left, the useful output on the right, and the waste (heat, sound) coming off the side, each labelled with its value.

Improving efficiency

Explain with a cause linked to the context and its effect: "Lubricate the gears (cause) to reduce friction, so less energy is lost as heat (effect)." Other examples: bearings, streamlining, insulation, reducing weight.

2. Kinetic energy depends on…

  1. Height
  2. Mass and velocity
  3. Current and time
  4. Temperature
Show answer
B: Mass and velocity. ½ m v².

3. A motor takes in 2000 J and gives 1500 J of useful work. Its efficiency is…

  1. 3500
  2. 1.33
  3. 0.75
  4. 500
Show answer
C: 0.75. 1500 ÷ 2000.

4. Unwanted energy in a gearbox is mainly lost as…

  1. Light
  2. Chemical energy
  3. Potential energy
  4. Heat and sound
Show answer
D: Heat and sound. Through friction.

5. The unit of power is…

  1. Watt
  2. Newton
  3. Joule
