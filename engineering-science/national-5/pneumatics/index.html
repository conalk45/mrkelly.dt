Pneumatics

13 cards · 7 quiz · 0 exam

Engineering Science · National 5 · mrkellydt.co.uk

Components

  • Single-acting cylinder: air outstrokes the piston; a spring returns it.
  • Double-acting cylinder: air drives the piston both out and in.
  • 3/2 valve: 3 ports, 2 states. Actuated by push button, lever, roller, plunger, solenoid, diaphragm (low-pressure signal from an air bleed) or pilot air; usually spring return.
  • 5/2 valve: 5 ports, 2 states; controls a double-acting cylinder. Often pilot-air operated in both directions, so it stays in its last state (memory).
  • Restrictor and uni-directional restrictor (UDR): control air flow. A UDR restricts flow one way only.
  • Reservoir: a tank that takes time to fill; with a restrictor it gives a time delay.
  • Shuttle valve: OR control. Two valves in series: AND control.

Why pneumatics?

  • Advantages: air is clean, so leaks don't contaminate food or medicine production; no sparks, so it is safe in flammable or explosive areas; fast, reliable and cheap to maintain; a cylinder can stall under overload without damage.
  • Disadvantages: needs a compressor; the exhaust is noisy; air compresses, so positions are less precise and forces are smaller than in hydraulics; leaks waste energy.
  • Safety: wear eye protection, switch the air off before changing a circuit, and never point compressed air at anyone.

Describing pneumatic circuits

Use the correct words: 3/2 valves actuate, 5/2 valves change state, pistons outstroke and instroke. E.g. "Valve 2 is actuated, so pilot air changes the state of valve 3 and cylinders A and B outstroke. Piston B actuates valve 4; pilot air changes valve 3 back and both cylinders instroke." All three course reports (2024–2025) criticised vague terms here.

Semi-automatic and fully automatic circuits

  • Semi-automatic: a push-button valve starts one cycle. Pilot air changes the state of the 5/2 valve and the piston outstrokes; at the end of its stroke it actuates a roller trip valve, which changes the 5/2 valve back, and the piston instrokes. The user must press again to repeat.
  • Fully automatic: a roller trip at each end of the stroke, so the piston keeps outstroking and instroking until the air is switched off or a stop valve is used.
  • A T-piece splits one air line into two, e.g. to send main air to two valves.

Speed control and time delays

  • To slow a piston, restrict the exhaust air leaving the cylinder, using a UDR in the exhaust line or a restrictor on the 5/2 valve's exhaust port. Restricting exhaust gives smoother, more controlled movement.
  • A time delay: a UDR feeding a reservoir before a pilot or diaphragm-operated valve. The smaller the restriction or the bigger the reservoir, the longer the delay.

Force, pressure and area

  • P = F ÷ A. Area of a piston A = π d² ÷ 4.
  • On the instroke of a double-acting cylinder, air pushes on the piston minus the rod: effective area = piston area − rod area. Square each diameter first, then subtract.
  • Work in N/mm² with areas in mm². Don't convert to m² unless asked: a 2025 error was giving N/m² without converting.
  • Don't use the diameter directly in P = F/A; calculate the area first.

Pressure in bar and worked examples

  • 1 bar = 0.1 N/mm², so 3 bar = 0.3 N/mm² and 0.5 N/mm² = 5 bar.
  • Force: a 40 mm cylinder at 3 bar: A = π × 20² = 1257 mm², F = 0.3 × 1257 = 380 N.
  • Pressure: 454 N from a 34 mm piston: A = π × 34² ÷ 4 = 907.9 mm², P = 454 ÷ 907.9 = 0.50 N/mm² = 5 bar.
  • Diameter: 400 N at 5 bar: A = F ÷ P = 400 ÷ 0.5 = 800 mm²; d = √(4A ÷ π) = √(3200 ÷ π) = 32 mm.
  • Instroke of a double-acting cylinder: piston 25 mm, rod 10 mm, 0.4 N/mm²: A = π × 12.5² − π × 5² = 412 mm², F = 0.4 × 412 = 165 N.
  • A single-acting cylinder's instroke is driven by its return spring, so you can't change its force with air pressure.
  • To increase the force: increase the pressure or use a larger piston.

What catches people out

  • Described a pneumatic circuit without the correct terms. 3/2 valve is actuated; 5/2 valve changes state; piston outstrokes or instrokes. N5 2024, 2025
  • Put the piston diameter straight into the pressure formula. Area = πr² (radius, not diameter). Then pressure = force ÷ area. N5 2024
  • Gave pressure in N/m² or Pa without converting mm to m. Use N/mm² unless the question says otherwise. N5 2024, 2025
  • Didn't spot AND control from two valves piped in series. Two 3/2 valves in series: both must be actuated. Two valves through a shuttle valve: OR control. N5 2025

Quick check

1. A circuit that keeps the piston going in and out until the air is switched off is…

  1. Semi-automatic
  2. Fully automatic
  3. Manual
  4. Sequential
Show answer
B: Fully automatic. Roller trips at both ends.

2. Which is an advantage of pneumatics?

  1. Very precise positioning
  2. No sparks, so safe near flammable materials
  3. Silent
  4. Larger forces than hydraulics
Show answer
B: No sparks, so safe near flammable materials. Air gives no spark risk.

3. How many ports and states does a 5/2 valve have?

  1. 5 states, 2 ports
  2. 5 ports, 2 states
  3. 2 ports, 5 states
  4. 3 ports, 2 states
Show answer
B: 5 ports, 2 states. Ports/states.

4. To slow a piston smoothly you should restrict…

  1. The supply air
  2. The exhaust air
  3. The pilot air
  4. The reservoir
Show answer
B: The exhaust air. Restrict the exhaust.

5. Two valves piped in series give…

  1. OR control
  2. AND control
  3. NOT control
  4. A time delay
Show answer
B: AND control. Both must be actuated.

6. A 12 mm diameter piston: area is about…

  1. 113 mm²
  2. 452 mm²
  3. 37.7 mm²
  4. 144 mm²
Show answer
A: 113 mm². π × 12² ÷ 4.

7. The correct way to describe a 5/2 valve operating is…

  1. It actuates
  2. It changes state
  3. It outstrokes
  4. It opens
Show answer
B: It changes state. 3/2 actuates; 5/2 changes state.
