Orthographic projection

8 cards · 6 quiz · 0 exam

Graphic Communication · Higher · mrkellydt.co.uk

Third-angle projection

Orthographic projection shows an object as separate flat (2D) views, each looking straight at one face. SQA uses third-angle projection.

  • Elevation: the main view, looking at the front.
  • Plan: looking down from above. In third angle it is drawn above the elevation.
  • End elevation: looking from the side. In third angle the view from the left is drawn on the left of the elevation, the view from the right on the right.

The views are related: they line up with each other, with feint projection lines carrying sizes between them. Heights match between elevation and end elevation; lengths match between elevation and plan.

The third-angle symbol (a cone frustum and two circles, circles on the right) goes in the title block to show which system was used.

Hidden detail

Edges you can't see from that viewpoint, such as holes or internal cut-outs, are drawn as thin dashed lines. Every view must show the hidden detail that exists in it: in the assignment, missing hidden detail costs the component mark.

Reading sizes from a drawing

Exam questions often ask you to work out a missing size. Use the dimensions that are shown and add or subtract: overall length minus the known parts, or a radius doubled for a diameter. Check which view shows the size you need: heights come from elevations, depths from the plan or end elevation.

True length and true shape

A surface that slopes away from you looks shorter than it really is in a normal view (it is foreshortened). Its true shape can be found by projecting at 90° to the sloping surface, so you look straight at it. Exam questions ask you to pick the true shape from several options: imagine looking square-on to the cut face.

Auxiliary views HIGHER

An auxiliary view is an extra view projected at 90° to an inclined surface, showing that surface's true shape. An auxiliary plan, for example, is projected from the elevation at right angles to the sloping face. It is used when features on a sloping face (such as holes) would otherwise appear distorted.

Tangency HIGHER

Where an arc blends smoothly into a straight line or another arc, the join is a tangent point. The arc's centre is always exactly one radius away from each line it touches, on the inside of the curve.

  • External radius (a rounded outside corner): the centre sits inside the material. A corner 15 from the datum with R2.5 has its centre at 15 − 2.5 = 12.5.
  • Internal radius (a fillet in an inside corner): the centre sits in the open space beside the material. A step face 4 from the datum with an R1.5 fillet has its centre at 4 + 1.5 = 5.5.

Sketch the corner and ask "which side of the line is the curve's inside?" before adding or subtracting. Almost every candidate got the 2024 tangency questions wrong, so practise this.

What catches people out

  • Tangency calculations answered incorrectly by almost all. Internal radius: offset inwards (subtract). External radius: offset outwards (add). Higher 2024

Quick check

1. In third-angle projection, the plan is drawn…

  1. Below the elevation
  2. Above the elevation
  3. To the left of the elevation
  4. On a separate sheet
Show answer
B: Above the elevation. Third angle: plan above.

2. Hidden detail is shown with…

  1. Thin dashed lines
  2. Thick continuous lines
  3. Chain lines
  4. Wavy lines
Show answer
A: Thin dashed lines. Dashed thin lines.

3. The third-angle symbol shows a cone frustum with its circles…

  1. On the left
  2. On the right
  3. Above
  4. Below
Show answer
B: On the right. Circles on the right = third angle.

4. To find the true shape of a sloping cut you look…

  1. From the front
  2. From above
  3. At 90° to the cut surface
  4. From below
Show answer
C: At 90° to the cut surface. Square-on to the surface.

5. An auxiliary view is used to…

  1. Show hidden detail
  2. Show the true shape of an inclined surface
  3. Show how parts assemble
  4. Add colour
Show answer
B: Show the true shape of an inclined surface. Projected at 90° to the inclined face.

6. An outside corner 15 from the datum is rounded R2. Its centre is…

  1. 15
  2. 17
  3. 13
  4. 30
Show answer
C: 13. External radius: centre inside the material, 15 − 2 = 13.
