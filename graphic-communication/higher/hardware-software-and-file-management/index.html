Advantages and limitations of CAD

  • Advantages: accurate; easy to edit and reuse; libraries save time; drawings and renders come from one model; files shared electronically and worldwide; models can be tested and simulated; links directly to CAM and 3D printing.
  • Limitations: cost of hardware, software licences and training; files can be lost, corrupted or incompatible; viruses and hacking; screen work can cause eye strain and repetitive strain injury; can slow down early, free idea generation.

3D printing

Benefits when prototyping or making products: no moulds or tooling; complex shapes and moving parts printed in one go; prints straight from the CAD file; easy to change and reprint; little waste because it is additive; accurate; can be customised for one-off products; the prototype can be tested. The 2024 N5 report: "it is cheap" or "it is quick" are wrong on their own.

Cloud computing, file management and rights HIGHER

  • Cloud storage and computing: files held on remote servers. You can access them from anywhere and on any device, which allows remote working; colleagues can share and collaborate; version history and backups prevent lost work; files can be encrypted; no physical storage is needed. It depends on an internet connection and subscription.
  • File management: sensible names, folders and versions, so work is found quickly and the right version is used.
  • Digital rights management (DRM): technology that controls how digital content is used and copied, protecting the creator's intellectual property. Watermarks on images stop others using or selling them without payment and advertise the owner.

Digital publishing

Online versions of a publication can include hyperlinks, video, animation and sound, links to social media, zoom and text resizing, translation and text-to-speech. They reach a wider audience and are easy to update. In exam answers, give context: "a link takes the customer straight to the shop to buy" scores; listing two different links with the same purpose doesn't score twice.
