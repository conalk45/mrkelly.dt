Notes

3D rendering techniques at Higher

  • Light source: direction, intensity and type of lighting.
  • Materials: realistic metals, plastics and woods.
  • Texture: surface detail such as brushed metal or wood grain.
  • Reflections: shiny surfaces reflecting their surroundings.
  • Shade/shadow: shading and cast shadows giving depth.
  • Sited environment: placing the model in a realistic scene (a kitchen, a street). It shows scale, how it fits its surroundings and different conditions (day, night, weather).

Describe each technique applied to the model: one-word answers don't score.

Why use rendered images?

  • Non-specialists, such as clients and customers, can understand them easily.
  • They show colours, materials and textures realistically.
  • They can be used for marketing before the product exists, saving the cost of prototypes and photography.
  • Changes of colour or material can be shown quickly.

Physical models and 3D printing

A 3D-printed model gives a physical, tactile product. It can be held and tested, its proportions can be evaluated, and it can be shown to the target market. Unskilled users can produce accurate, complex models with little waste. "Cheap" and "quick" on their own don't score: say why or compared with what.

Key terms

Sited environment
Placing a 3D model in a realistic scene to show scale and context.
Higher rendering techniques
Light source, materials, texture, reflections, shade/shadow, sited environment.
Decal
An image such as a logo applied to the surface of a 3D model.
Why render for a client?
Easy for non-specialists to understand; shows materials and colours realistically.
