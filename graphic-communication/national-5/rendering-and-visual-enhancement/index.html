CAD rendering at National 5 N5

The three techniques that make a CAD model look realistic are materials, surface texture and light source. A decal (an image, such as a logo, applied to a surface) is also credited. "Colour", "highlights" and "shadows" are not accepted: highlights and shadows are by-products of lighting and materials.

Why use rendered images?

  • Non-specialists, such as clients and customers, can understand them easily.
  • They show colours, materials and textures realistically.
  • They can be used for marketing before the product exists, saving the cost of prototypes and photography.
  • Changes of colour or material can be shown quickly.

Physical models and 3D printing

A 3D-printed model gives a physical, tactile product. It can be held and tested, its proportions can be evaluated, and it can be shown to the target market. Unskilled users can produce accurate, complex models with little waste. "Cheap" and "quick" on their own don't score: say why or compared with what.

What catches people out

  • Gave "colour", "highlights" or "shadows" as CAD rendering techniques. Materials, surface texture, light source (decals accepted). N5 2024, 2025
