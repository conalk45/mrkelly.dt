Assemblies, constraints & file types

3 cards · 3 quiz · 0 exam

Graphic Communication · National 5 · mrkellydt.co.uk

Assembly constraints

  • Mate: two faces brought together, facing each other (touching).
  • Align (flush): two faces made level, facing the same way.
  • Centre axis (axis to axis): the centre lines of two round features lined up, e.g. a pin in a hole.

Typical order: centre axis to line parts up, then mate to bring faces together, then align to finish. Use the exact terms: the 2024 N5 report says most candidates didn't give "centre axis".

Producing drawings from CAD

CAD can generate orthographic, sectional and pictorial views straight from the 3D model, so views are always consistent and update when the model changes. Never type over a wrong dimension on a production drawing: fix the model instead. In the assignment an overridden dimension scores 0 for that component.

What catches people out

  • Didn't give "centre axis" as a constraint. Centre axis lines up round features; mate brings faces together; align makes faces flush. N5 2024

Key terms

MateTwo faces brought together facing each other.
AlignTwo faces made flush, facing the same way.
Centre axisLines up the axes of two round features.
