Elements and principles

TermWhat it means in a layout
LineLines that separate, lead the eye, connect or underline items
ColourUsed for mood, contrast, harmony and to link items (see Colour)
AlignmentItems lined up with each other: left, right, centre, top, bottom or justified. Always name the type
ContrastDifferences that make items stand out: colour, size, shape, weight of font, light and dark
DominanceThe item that stands out most and is seen first (the focal point), usually the largest or brightest
DepthMaking items seem to sit in front of others: layering, overlap, drop shadow, gradients
UnityItems look like they belong together: repeated colours, fonts and shapes, and grouping related items

How to answer layout questions

  • Be specific: name the actual items. "The red heading contrasts with the black background" scores; "contrast makes it stand out" doesn't.
  • Two or more items for unity, contrast, alignment and depth: these principles are relationships between items.
  • Name the alignment type: "the body text and heading are left aligned".
  • Explain = cause + effect: "The title is the largest text (cause), so it dominates and is read first (effect)." Course reports say most candidates give only the effect.
  • Don't repeat the same point for different parts of a question.

What catches people out

  • Didn't explain the effect the title had, or how alignment enhanced the layout. Name the items and the effect: "the title is the largest item, so it dominates". N5 2024

Key terms

DominanceThe item that stands out most and is seen first.
ContrastDifferences in colour, size, shape or weight that make items stand out.
UnityItems look like they belong together: repeated colour, font, shape.
DepthItems appear to be in front of others: layering, overlap, drop shadow.
AlignmentItems lined up; always name the type: left, right, centre, top, bottom.
