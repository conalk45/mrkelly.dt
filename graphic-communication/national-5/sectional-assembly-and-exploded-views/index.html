Sectional, assembly & exploded views

4 cards · 3 quiz · 0 exam

Graphic Communication · National 5 · mrkellydt.co.uk

Sectional views

A sectional view shows the inside of an object as if it has been cut through along a cutting plane. The cutting plane is shown on another view with arrows for the viewing direction and letters, and the section is labelled SECTION A–A.

  • Hatching: thin lines at 45°, evenly spaced, on the cut surfaces only.
  • Different parts in an assembly are hatched in different directions or spacings; the same part keeps the same hatching everywhere.
  • Not hatched when cut along their length: webs and ribs, shafts, nuts, bolts, screws, washers, pins, keys and threads.
  • Hidden detail is usually left out of sectional views.

Assembly drawings

An assembly drawing shows how components fit together, often as orthographic views with a section. In the assignment it must be accurate with no overlaps or gaps, views related and correctly orientated, and sections taken where the task says.

Exploded views

An exploded view pulls the parts of an assembly apart along their assembly lines. A good exploded view has:

  • parts in the correct order of assembly;
  • parts aligned along the axis they assemble on, often with a centre line or "trail";
  • even spacing, so no parts overlap and every part can be seen;
  • parts labelled or numbered to match a parts list.

Exam questions show an exploded view and ask for examples of good or poor practice: look for overlapping parts, misalignment, wrong order or hidden parts.

Key terms

HatchingThin 45° lines on cut surfaces of a sectional view.
Which parts are not hatched?Webs, ribs, shafts, nuts, bolts, screws, washers, pins and threads cut lengthways.
SECTION A–AThe label for the view taken at cutting plane A–A.
Good exploded viewCorrect order, aligned, evenly spaced, no overlaps, all parts visible.

Quick check

1. Which of these is NOT hatched when cut lengthways?

  1. A casing
  2. A bolt
  3. A base plate
  4. A housing
Show answer
B: A bolt. Bolts, nuts, shafts and webs are left unhatched.

2. Hatching lines are normally drawn at…

  1. 30°
  2. 45°
  3. 60°
  4. 90°
Show answer
B: 45°. 45°, thin, evenly spaced.

3. Which is a feature of a good exploded view?

  1. Parts overlap to save space
  2. Parts are aligned along assembly lines
  3. Parts are shown in random order
  4. Hidden detail on every part
Show answer
B: Parts are aligned along assembly lines. Aligned, ordered, spaced.
