Sustainability & recycling

7 cards · 4 quiz · 0 exam

Practical Woodworking · National 5 · mrkellydt.co.uk

Sustainable timber

  • FSC (Forest Stewardship Council) and PEFC labels show wood comes from responsibly managed forests, where trees are replanted.
  • Softwoods grow faster than hardwoods, so they're replaced sooner.
  • Buying local timber cuts transport emissions.
  • Benefits of sustainably sourced wood: forests are replanted, wildlife habitats are protected, there's a long-term supply, and trees keep absorbing CO₂.

Making a product more environmentally friendly

  • Use FSC-certified or reclaimed wood.
  • Use manufactured boards made from waste wood.
  • Plan the cutting list to reduce waste.
  • Use water-based finishes and glues, which give off fewer harmful fumes.
  • Design it to last, to be repaired, or to be taken apart for recycling (e.g. KD fittings).
  • Use offcuts for smaller parts, and recycle or reuse what's left.

Workshop recycling

  • Keep useful offcuts in an offcut bin for small jobs.
  • Sawdust and shavings can be used for animal bedding, compost or biomass fuel.
  • Separate metal, plastic and wood waste.
  • Dispose of finishes and solvents safely, never down the sink.

Chipboard vs pine (environmental)

Chipboard uses waste wood (an advantage), but contains resins and glues that make it harder to recycle and can give off fumes (a disadvantage). Pine is a natural, renewable, biodegradable material, but whole trees are used.

What catches people out

  • Couldn't give benefits of sustainably sourced wood. Trees are replanted, habitats protected, a long-term supply. N5 2024 paper

Quick check

1. What does the FSC logo show?

  1. The wood is waterproof and needs no finish
  2. The wood is a hardwood, not a softwood
  3. The wood is the cheapest available
  4. The wood comes from responsibly managed forests
Show answer
D: The wood comes from responsibly managed forests. FSC forests are replanted and managed.

2. Which would make a product more environmentally friendly?

  1. Planning the cutting list to reduce waste
  2. Using a solvent-based varnish finish
  3. Using an imported tropical hardwood
  4. Using extra glue in every joint
Show answer
A: Planning the cutting list to reduce waste. Less waste means fewer trees used.

3. What is an environmental advantage of chipboard?

  1. It's waterproof
  2. It's made from waste wood
  3. It has attractive grain
  4. It contains no glue
Show answer
B: It's made from waste wood. Chipboard uses chips that would otherwise be wasted.

4. How should you dispose of leftover solvent finish?

  1. Down the sink with plenty of water
  2. In the general waste bin
  3. Safely, following workshop procedures
  4. Poured onto the ground outside
Show answer
C: Safely, following workshop procedures. Solvents harm water and wildlife.
