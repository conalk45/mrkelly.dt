Notes

Saws

SawUse
Tenon sawStraight cuts in joints; the stiff back keeps the blade straight
Dovetail sawFiner, smaller tenon saw for delicate joints
Coping sawCurves and removing waste from joints; thin replaceable blade
Panel saw / hand sawCutting larger boards and planks to size

Planes

PlaneUse
Jack planeGeneral planing to size and straightening edges
Smoothing planeFinal smooth finish on surfaces
Block planeEnd grain and small work; low blade angle
Rebate planeCuts a rebate (a step) along an edge; uses a fence and depth stop
Plough planeCuts a groove along the grain, e.g. for a panel or drawer base
Bull-nose planeCleans up rebates and stopped work right into corners

Plane with the grain to avoid tearing the surface. When planing end grain, work in from both ends, or clamp a waste block on, so the far corner doesn't split.

Chisels and shaping tools

  • Firmer chisel: strong, general-purpose, can be struck with a mallet.
  • Bevel-edged chisel: bevelled sides get into corners, e.g. dovetails and housings.
  • Mortise chisel: thick and strong for chopping mortises with a mallet.
  • Spokeshave: shapes and smooths curved edges (after a coping saw).
  • Hand router: levels the bottom of a housing or recess to an exact depth.

Always keep both hands behind the cutting edge. Strike chisels with a mallet, never a hammer.

Other bench tools

  • Hammers: Warrington (cross pein) for small nails; claw hammer for large nails and pulling them out. Mallet for chisels and assembly.
  • Screwdrivers: match the tip to the screw head (slotted, Phillips, Pozidriv).
  • Hand drill and brace: drill holes; a brace with an auger bit drills larger holes. Clamp a waste block underneath to stop the underside splintering.
  • Bradawl: makes a starting hole for screws. Countersink bit: lets screw heads sit flush.
  • Bench vice: holds work securely, with the work low to stop vibration.

Quick check

1. Which plane cuts a groove for a drawer base?

  1. Jack plane
  2. Plough plane
  3. Block plane
  4. Smoothing plane
Show answer
B: Plough plane. A plough plane cuts grooves along the grain.

2. Which two hand tools would cut and smooth a curved edge?

  1. Tenon saw and jack plane
  2. Coping saw and spokeshave
  3. Panel saw and chisel
  4. Dovetail saw and file
Show answer
B: Coping saw and spokeshave. Coping saw to cut the curve, spokeshave to smooth it.

3. Which tool levels the bottom of a housing joint?

  1. Bradawl
  2. Hand router
  3. Marking gauge
  4. Mallet
Show answer
B: Hand router. The hand router cuts to a set depth.

4. Why does a tenon saw have a heavy back?

  1. To make it heavier
  2. To keep the thin blade stiff and straight
  3. To protect your hand
  4. For decoration
Show answer
B: To keep the thin blade stiff and straight. The back stops the blade bending.

5. What should you strike a chisel with?

  1. Claw hammer
  2. Warrington hammer
  3. Mallet
  4. Spanner
Show answer
C: Mallet. A mallet won't damage the handle.
