Notes

What is a flat frame?

A flat frame is made from lengths of wood joined at the corners and middle, like a door, a table frame or a chair back. The upright parts are stiles; the cross pieces are rails.

Corner joints

JointNotes
ButtSimplest; weak unless reinforced with dowels, screws or a corner block
Mitre45° on both parts; hides end grain, so it looks neat (picture frames); weak without reinforcement
DowelButt joint strengthened with glued dowels; needs accurate drilling (use a jig)
Corner halvingHalf the thickness removed from each part; easy to cut, good glue area
Corner bridleAn open slot and tongue; strong, large glue area
Haunched mortise and tenonThe haunch stops the rail twisting and fills the groove end in a panelled frame; very strong at a corner

T joints and cross joints

  • T halving and T bridle: a rail joining part-way along a stile.
  • Stub mortise and tenon: the tenon doesn't go all the way through, so it's hidden.
  • Through mortise and tenon: the tenon goes right through: very strong, and can be wedged.
  • Cross halving: two parts crossing, each cut half way.
  • Dovetail halving: a halving with angled sides that resists being pulled apart.

A tenon is normally one third of the thickness of the wood.

Choosing a flat-frame joint

  • Strength needed: mortise and tenon or bridle for chairs and tables; halving for light frames.
  • Appearance: mitre hides end grain; stub tenon is hidden; through tenon shows.
  • Skill and time: halving is quickest; mortise and tenon takes longest.
  • Advantages of traditional joints over knock-down fittings: no extra parts to buy, they look attractive and show craft skill, a large glue area makes them permanent, and they don't work loose.

Cutting a corner halving (stages)

  1. Mark the width of the joining piece across both parts with a try square and marking knife.
  2. Set a marking gauge to half the thickness and gauge from the face side on both parts.
  3. Shade the waste.
  4. Saw down the grain on the waste side of the gauge line with a tenon saw.
  5. Saw across the shoulder line to remove the waste.
  6. Clean up with a chisel, then test the fit.

Quick check

1. Which joint hides the end grain at a picture-frame corner?

  1. Butt
  2. Corner halving
  3. Mitre
  4. Bridle
Show answer
C: Mitre. Mitres meet at 45°, so no end grain shows.

2. What does the haunch on a haunched mortise and tenon do?

  1. Decorates the joint
  2. Stops the rail twisting and fills the groove end
  3. Makes it knock-down
  4. Holds the glue
Show answer
B: Stops the rail twisting and fills the groove end. It adds strength at a corner and fills the panel groove.

3. Which joint is quickest to cut for a light frame?

  1. Through mortise and tenon
  2. Halving
  3. Dovetail halving
  4. Haunched mortise and tenon
Show answer
B: Halving. Halvings are quick and simple.

4. Which halving joint resists being pulled apart?

  1. Cross halving
  2. T halving
  3. Dovetail halving
  4. Corner halving
Show answer
C: Dovetail halving. The angled sides lock it.

5. How thick is a tenon usually?

  1. Half the wood
  2. One third of the wood
  3. One quarter of the wood
  4. The full thickness
Show answer
B: One third of the wood. The ratio is about one third.

6. Which is an advantage of a traditional joint over a knock-down fitting?

  1. It can be taken apart
  2. It needs no extra parts to buy
  3. It's quicker to make
  4. It needs no glue
Show answer
B: It needs no extra parts to buy. Knock-down fittings are bought parts; traditional joints are cut from the wood.
