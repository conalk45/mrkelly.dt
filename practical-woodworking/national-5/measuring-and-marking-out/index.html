Notes

Measuring tools

  • Steel rule: accurate measuring of shorter lengths (300 mm, 600 mm, 1 m).
  • Tape measure: long lengths and the alternative when a rule is too short.
  • Calipers: outside calipers check diameters when turning; inside calipers check holes.

Marking-out tools

ToolUse
Try squareMarks lines at 90° to an edge, and checks squareness
Mitre squareMarks 45° lines for mitre joints
Sliding bevelSet to any angle to mark or check angles, e.g. dovetails
Marking gaugeA spur scribes a line parallel to an edge, along the grain
Cutting gaugeA small blade cuts a clean line across the grain without tearing
Mortise gaugeTwo spurs mark both sides of a mortise or tenon at once
Marking knifeCuts a fine line across the grain for accurate sawing
PencilRough marking and marking waste
TemplateA pattern for marking identical shapes quickly and accurately

Good marking-out practice

  • Choose and mark a face side and face edge (the loop and the tick). Measure and square from these every time.
  • Mark out matching parts together (e.g. both stiles side by side) so joints line up.
  • Shade the waste so you saw on the correct side of the line.
  • Work to ±1 mm: the practical activity tolerance for marking out and cutting.
  • Templates save time when the same shape is needed many times, and make every part the same, because you only measure once.

Your marking out is assessed before you cut, so you have a chance to correct it.

Key terms

Quick check

1. Which tool marks a line across the grain without tearing the fibres?

  1. Marking gauge
  2. Cutting gauge
  3. Mortise gauge
  4. Pencil
Show answer
B: Cutting gauge. The cutting gauge has a small blade instead of a spur.

2. Which tool would you set to mark a 45° mitre?

  1. Try square
  2. Mitre square or sliding bevel
  3. Marking gauge
  4. Calipers
Show answer
B: Mitre square or sliding bevel. A mitre square is fixed at 45°; a sliding bevel can be set to it.

3. Why mark out the two stiles of a frame together?

  1. It looks neater
  2. The joints line up exactly
  3. It uses less pencil
  4. It's required by law
Show answer
B: The joints line up exactly. Marking matching parts together keeps them identical.

4. Which tool marks both sides of a mortise at once?

  1. Mortise gauge
  2. Marking knife
  3. Try square
  4. Steel rule
Show answer
A: Mortise gauge. It has two spurs, one fixed and one adjustable.

5. What is the tolerance for marking out and cutting?

  1. ±0.5 mm
  2. ±1 mm
  3. ±3 mm
  4. ±5 mm
Show answer
B: ±1 mm. Overall sizes are ±3 mm; marking out and cutting are ±1 mm.
