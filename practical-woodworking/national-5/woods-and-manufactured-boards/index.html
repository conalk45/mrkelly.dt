Softwoods and hardwoods

Softwoods come from conifers (needles, cones, evergreen). They grow quickly, so they're cheaper and more sustainable. Hardwoods come from broad-leaved trees; they grow slowly, are usually harder and more expensive.

WoodTypeProperties and uses
PineSoftwoodLight colour, easy to work, knots; cheap furniture, frames, school projects
CedarSoftwoodReddish, natural oils make it durable outdoors; sheds, cladding, garden furniture
LarchSoftwoodTough and durable outdoors; fencing, boats, cladding
OakHardwoodVery strong, hard, durable, attractive grain; quality furniture, flooring, outdoor use
AshHardwoodTough and flexible, absorbs shock; tool handles, sports equipment
BeechHardwoodHard, close grain, turns well; mallets, chairs, toys, workbenches
MahoganyHardwoodReddish-brown, easy to work, polishes well; quality furniture
MerantiHardwoodReddish, fairly cheap; window frames, doors, mouldings

Manufactured boards

BoardMade fromProperties and uses
PlywoodThin layers (veneers) glued with grain at 90°, odd number of layersStrong in all directions, won't split; drawer bases, backs, exterior grades for outdoors
MDFFine wood fibres and resin, pressedSmooth, no grain, machines and paints well; edges absorb water; dust is harmful
ChipboardWood chips and resinCheap, often veneered or laminated; edges crumble; flat-pack furniture with KD fittings
BlockboardStrips of softwood between veneersStiff, doesn't sag; shelves, worktops
HardboardCompressed fibres, one smooth faceCheap and thin; drawer bases, cabinet backs

Advantages of boards: available in large sizes, stable (don't warp or shrink), cheaper, use waste wood. You can identify them from their edge: layers (plywood), chips (chipboard), strips (blockboard), uniform fibres (MDF).

Choosing a wood

When asked to choose a wood and justify it, name the wood and link a property to the use, e.g. "Cedar, because its natural oils make it durable outdoors, so the bird box won't rot." Don't just repeat the table: compare and explain.

Dowel rod

Dowel rod is round hardwood (often ramin or beech) in standard diameters (6, 8, 10 mm…). Used for dowel joints, pegs and rails.

What catches people out

  • Couldn't identify manufactured boards from their construction. Look at the edge: layers, chips, strips or fibres. N5 2025 paper
  • Repeated a data table when choosing a hardwood. Compare the woods and link a property to the use. N5 2025 paper

Quick check

1. Which wood is naturally durable outdoors because of its oils?

  1. Pine
  2. Cedar
  3. Beech
  4. MDF
Show answer
B: Cedar. Cedar's oils resist rot.

2. Which board has layers with the grain at 90° to each other?

  1. MDF
  2. Chipboard
  3. Plywood
  4. Hardboard
Show answer
C: Plywood. Cross-graining makes plywood strong in all directions.

3. Which wood suits a tool handle that must absorb shock?

  1. Ash
  2. Mahogany
  3. Pine
  4. Meranti
Show answer
A: Ash. Ash is tough and flexible.

4. Which board's edges crumble and are best joined with KD fittings?

  1. Plywood
  2. Blockboard
  3. Chipboard
  4. Oak
Show answer
C: Chipboard. Chipboard edges break up.

5. Which is a hardwood?

  1. Larch
  2. Pine
  3. Beech
  4. Cedar
Show answer
C: Beech. Beech comes from a broad-leaved tree.

6. Which is an advantage of manufactured boards?

  1. Attractive grain
  2. Available in large, stable sheets
  3. Always waterproof
  4. Always stronger than solid wood
Show answer
B: Available in large, stable sheets. Boards come in large sizes and don't warp.
