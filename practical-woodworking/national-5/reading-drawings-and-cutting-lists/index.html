Reading drawings & cutting lists

8 cards · 5 quiz · 0 exam

Practical Woodworking · National 5 · mrkellydt.co.uk

Working drawings

A working drawing gives every size you need to make the product. In the practical activity you must work to the drawings exactly: you can't be given extra drawings or dimensions.

  • Orthographic projection: separate flat views (elevation, end elevation, plan) lined up with each other.
  • Pictorial drawing: a 3D view (e.g. isometric) that shows what the product looks like.
  • Exploded view: parts pulled apart to show how they fit together.

Dimensions and symbols

  • All sizes are in millimetres.
  • Linear dimensions show lengths; radial dimensions use R (R10 = radius 10 mm); diametric use Ø (Ø20 = diameter 20 mm); angular dimensions show degrees.
  • Scale: 1:2 means the drawing is half full size; 2:1 is twice full size; 1:1 is full size.
  • Ratio dimensions: e.g. a tenon is usually one third of the thickness of the wood.

Cutting lists

A cutting list lists every part with its length × breadth × thickness, the number needed and the material. Length is always given first, along the grain.

To find the total length of timber: multiply each part's length by how many you need, add them up, and allow for waste at the ends. Laying parts out carefully on the board reduces waste.

In the practical activity, lengths and breadths must never change. Thickness can change only if the exact size isn't available, and then the drawings must be amended.

What catches people out

  • Couldn't calculate total timber length from a drawing. Multiply each length by the number needed, then add them up. N5 2024 paper
  • Couldn't explain how to mark out to reduce waste. Nest parts closely and work from one edge. N5 2024 paper
  • Couldn't identify a centre line or the diameter symbol. Centre line: thin chain. Ø means diameter. N5 2024 paper
  • Used a different thickness of wood without amending the drawings. If the thickness changes, the drawings must be amended or measuring and marking marks are not awarded. QVSR 2025

Quick check

1. What does Ø25 mean on a drawing?

  1. Radius 25 mm
  2. Diameter 25 mm
  3. 25 degrees
  4. Depth 25 mm
Show answer
B: Diameter 25 mm. Ø is the diameter symbol; R is radius.

2. Which line type shows hidden detail?

  1. Thick continuous
  2. Thin chain
  3. Thin dashed
  4. Thin continuous
Show answer
C: Thin dashed. Hidden edges are thin dashed lines.

3. A drawing at 1:2 is…

  1. Twice full size
  2. Full size
  3. A quarter full size
  4. Half full size
Show answer
D: Half full size. 1:2 means 1 mm on the drawing is 2 mm on the real object.

4. On a cutting list, which size comes first?

  1. Length
  2. Breadth
  3. Thickness
  4. Diameter
Show answer
A: Length. Length × breadth × thickness.

5. In the practical activity, which sizes must never be altered?

  1. The thickness of each part only
  2. Lengths and breadths
  3. The overall height of the product
  4. None: any size can be changed
Show answer
B: Lengths and breadths. Only thickness may change, and the drawings must then be amended.
