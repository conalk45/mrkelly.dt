Notes

What is a carcase?

A carcase is a box-like construction made from wide boards, like a cabinet, bookcase, drawer or storage box. Joints must hold the boards square and carry shelves.

Carcase joints

JointNotes
ButtSimple but weak; needs nails, screws, dowels or blocks
Corner rebate (lap)A rebate in one board takes the end of the other: better glue area, hides some end grain, quick to cut
Through housingA groove across one board takes a shelf; the joint shows at the front
Stopped housingThe groove stops short of the front edge, so the joint is hidden: neater, more work
DowelDowels in the edge and face; strong and hidden; needs accurate drilling

Cutting a stopped housing (stages)

  1. Mark the shelf position across the board with a try square and marking knife, using the shelf to get the width.
  2. Gauge the depth (about one third of the board thickness) and the stop on the front.
  3. Chop out a small recess at the stopped end with a chisel so the saw has room to move.
  4. Saw down both sides of the housing to the depth line with a tenon saw.
  5. Remove the waste with a chisel, then level the bottom with a hand router.
  6. Cut a notch in the front of the shelf to fit the stop, and test the fit.

Choosing a carcase joint

  • Shelves: through housing (quick) or stopped housing (hidden).
  • Corners of a box: corner rebate or dowel.
  • Manufactured boards (chipboard, MDF) suit dowels and knock-down fittings better than traditional joints, because their edges crumble.

Quick check

1. Which housing joint can't be seen from the front?

  1. Through housing
  2. Stopped housing
  3. Corner rebate
  4. Butt
Show answer
B: Stopped housing. The groove stops short of the front edge.

2. Why chop a recess at the end of a stopped housing first?

  1. To make it deeper
  2. To give the saw room to move
  3. To hold the glue
  4. To decorate it
Show answer
B: To give the saw room to move. The saw can't run out of the stopped end.

3. Which joint is best for the corners of a simple box?

  1. Mortise and tenon
  2. Corner rebate
  3. Cross halving
  4. Bridle
Show answer
B: Corner rebate. Corner rebate is a carcase corner joint.

4. Which board joins best with dowels rather than housings?

  1. Pine
  2. Oak
  3. Chipboard
  4. Beech
Show answer
C: Chipboard. Chipboard edges crumble when cut into joints.
