Notes

Preparing the surface

  1. Plane surfaces smooth (smoothing plane).
  2. Scrape with a cabinet scraper to remove tears and plane marks, especially on difficult grain.
  3. Stop (fill small holes and cracks with wood stopper/filler matched to the colour of the wood) and let it dry.
  4. Sand with abrasive paper wrapped round a cork block, with the grain, working from coarse to medium to fine.
  5. Remove dust with a brush or tack cloth before finishing.

Glass paper is cheaper; garnet paper is harder and lasts longer. Grades: coarse, medium, fine.

In the practical activity, don't put filler or finish over joints before they've been verified.

Finishes

FinishProperties
Varnish (polyurethane)Tough, waterproof film; clear or coloured; gloss, satin or matt. Several coats, rubbed down between coats
StainChanges the colour of the wood but doesn't protect it, so it needs varnish or wax on top. Advantage over varnish: changes colour while showing the grain, and it soaks in so it doesn't chip
WaxSoft sheen, enhances grain, easy to apply; little protection; needs re-applying
Danish oilSoaks in and hardens; natural look; good for hardwoods and turned work
Linseed oilTraditional oil; slow drying; outdoor use and cricket bats
Vegetable oilFood-safe: chopping boards, salad bowls and kitchen utensils

Applying a finish

  • Apply with a brush or lint-free cloth, with the grain, in thin even coats.
  • Let each coat dry, then rub down lightly with fine abrasive or wire wool before the next.
  • Cover every surface for full marks, with no runs, drips or missed areas.
  • Work in a ventilated area; wear gloves for stains and oils. Oily rags can self-ignite: lay them flat to dry or put them in a metal bin.

Quick check

1. Which finish is food-safe for a chopping board?

  1. Varnish
  2. Vegetable oil
  3. Stain
  4. Wax polish with solvent
Show answer
B: Vegetable oil. Vegetable oil is safe in contact with food.

2. In what order should abrasive paper be used?

  1. Fine to coarse
  2. Coarse to fine
  3. Medium only
  4. Any order
Show answer
B: Coarse to fine. Each grade removes the scratches of the previous one.

3. What does stain do?

  1. Protects the wood from water
  2. Changes the colour of the wood
  3. Fills holes
  4. Hardens the surface
Show answer
B: Changes the colour of the wood. It needs a protective finish on top.

4. What is "stopping"?

  1. Removing tear-out with a scraper
  2. Filling small holes and cracks
  3. Sanding the end grain
  4. Applying the last coat
Show answer
B: Filling small holes and cracks. Not to be confused with scraping.

5. Why rub down between coats of varnish?

  1. To remove all the varnish
  2. To give a key and remove dust nibs
  3. To change the colour
  4. To make it dry faster
Show answer
B: To give a key and remove dust nibs. It gives a smooth surface for the next coat.
