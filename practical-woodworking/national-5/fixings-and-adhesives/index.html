Notes

Nails and screws

  • Nails: quick, but can split wood and pull out. Oval nails split less; panel pins are for thin sheet; nail at an angle (dovetail nailing) to grip better.
  • Screws: stronger than nails and can be removed. Countersunk heads sit flush; round heads sit on top. Drill a pilot hole and a clearance hole so the wood doesn't split.

Brackets, blocks and knock-down fittings

  • Angle brackets and corner blocks: strengthen joints inside frames and carcases.
  • Knock-down (KD) fittings: bought fittings (e.g. cam locks, block joints, cross dowels) used for flat-pack furniture.
KD advantagesKD disadvantages
Can be taken apart and moved; quick to assemble with simple tools; suit manufactured boards; furniture can be sold flat-packedCan work loose; extra parts to buy; less attractive; usually not stronger than a glued joint

A common wrong answer: KD fittings are not an advantage for strength.

Adhesives

  • PVA: white wood glue for interior work. Strong, cheap, easy to clean up with water.
  • Exterior (waterproof) PVA or polyurethane / resin glues: for outdoor products like garden furniture and bird boxes.
  • Contact adhesive: for laminates and veneers; bonds on contact.
  • Hot glue: quick for models and jigs, not for furniture.

Quick check

1. Which glue suits a garden bench?

  1. Standard PVA
  2. Exterior (waterproof) glue
  3. Hot glue
  4. Glue stick
Show answer
B: Exterior (waterproof) glue. Outdoor products need waterproof glue.

2. Which is a real advantage of knock-down fittings?

  1. Stronger than glued joints
  2. Can be taken apart and moved
  3. Never work loose
  4. No tools needed ever
Show answer
B: Can be taken apart and moved. Strength is not an advantage; being able to dismantle is.

3. Why drill a pilot hole before screwing?

  1. To make it look neat
  2. To stop the wood splitting
  3. To use a shorter screw
  4. To hide the screw
Show answer
B: To stop the wood splitting. It guides the screw and stops splitting.

4. Which screw head sits flush with the surface?

  1. Round head
  2. Countersunk
  3. Pan head
  4. Dome head
Show answer
B: Countersunk. Countersunk heads sit in a cone-shaped recess.
