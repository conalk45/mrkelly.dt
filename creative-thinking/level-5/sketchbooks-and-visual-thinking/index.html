Sketchbooks & visual thinking

4 cards · 3 quiz · 0 exam

Creative Thinking · Level 5 · mrkellydt.co.uk

Your sketchbook is your evidence

Your sketchbook (and digital pages) is where assessors see your thinking. It doesn't need to be beautiful; it needs to be full of thinking.

  • Date your pages and keep them in order so the process is clear.
  • Mix sketches, photos, cuttings, samples, notes and printouts.
  • Use headings that match the five elements so the assessor can find each one.

Annotation

Annotation is the notes beside your work. Good annotation explains why, not just what:

  • Weak: "This is my idea."
  • Strong: "I rounded the edges after two Primary 3 pupils said the corners looked sharp. This makes it feel friendlier and safer."

Use arrows to link notes to the exact part you're talking about.

Visual communication basics

  • Thumbnail sketches: small, fast sketches to explore lots of options.
  • Colour, contrast and composition make your pages and final ideas readable.
  • Typography: choose fonts that suit the message and are readable.
  • Photography and film can record tests and present ideas in context.

What catches people out

  • Annotations that only say what. Explain why you made each decision. Portfolio guidance

Key terms

AnnotationNotes on your work explaining what and especially why
Thumbnail sketchA small, quick sketch to explore an option
Strong annotationExplains why you made a decision, with evidence
Sketchbook orderDated pages in order so the process is clear

Quick check

1. Which annotation is strongest?

  1. Idea 3.
  2. I like this one.
  3. I made the text bigger because users at the back of the hall couldn't read it in testing.
  4. Nice colours.
Show answer
C: I made the text bigger because users at the back of the hall couldn't read it in testing.. It explains why, with evidence.

2. What matters most in a sketchbook?

  1. Neat drawing
  2. Visible thinking
  3. Expensive materials
  4. Lots of blank space
Show answer
B: Visible thinking. Assessors look for the thinking.

3. What are thumbnail sketches for?

  1. Final presentations
  2. Exploring lots of options quickly
  3. Technical drawings
  4. Measuring
Show answer
B: Exploring lots of options quickly. Quick and small.
