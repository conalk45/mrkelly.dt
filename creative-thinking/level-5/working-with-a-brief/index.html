Notes

What a brief is

A brief is a set of instructions from a client describing the problem, who it's for and any limits. Daydream Believers briefs come from real companies and organisations.

Analysing a brief

  • Highlight key words and pick out the actual problem.
  • Who is the client, and who is the user or audience?
  • What must the outcome be (a space, product, campaign, service, brand)?
  • Constraints: budget, size, time, materials, location, rules.
  • Success: how will you know it worked?
  • Turn it into research questions and a "How might we…?"

Types of brief

  • Spaces: design a school, hospital ward, music venue or workplace.
  • Campaigns: advertising or awareness campaigns, e.g. kindness, mental health, sustainability.
  • Products and services: something for a particular group of users.
  • Brands: build a brand, its identity and how it sells.
  • Communities: decide how a community should use a resource.

Key terms

Brief
Instructions from a client describing the problem, users and limits
Client
The person or organisation who sets the brief
User / audience
The people the outcome is for
Constraint
A limit: budget, size, time, materials, rules
Success criteria
How you'll know the idea worked

Quick check

1. What is a constraint in a brief?

  1. A colour you like
  2. A limit such as budget or size
  3. The final idea
  4. A research method
Show answer
B: A limit such as budget or size. Constraints limit the solution.

2. What should you do first with a new brief?

  1. Start making the final product
  2. Analyse it: key words, users, constraints
  3. Choose a font
  4. Present it
Show answer
B: Analyse it: key words, users, constraints. Understand the problem first.

3. Who is the "client"?

  1. The person who sets the brief
  2. The user
  3. The teacher always
  4. The assessor
Show answer
A: The person who sets the brief. The client sets the brief; the user uses the outcome.
