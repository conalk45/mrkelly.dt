Notes

Evaluating your work

Evaluation means judging your own work honestly: what worked, what didn't, and why. It happens throughout, not just at the end.

  • Refer back to the brief and your research: did the idea solve the real problem?
  • Use evidence: test results, feedback, comparisons.
  • Give strengths and weaknesses, and how you'd improve it.

Creative bravery

Creative bravery means taking risks and being honest about them:

  • trying an unusual idea rather than the safe one
  • admitting when something didn't work and explaining what you learned
  • challenging your own assumptions
  • being open to criticism and changing direction

Reflective writing

A simple structure for reflection: What? (what I did) → So what? (what it showed, why it matters) → Now what? (what I'll do next).

Avoid empty phrases like "I think it went well." Say what went well, how you know, and why.

Key terms

Evaluation
Honest judgement of your work using evidence
Creative bravery
Taking risks, and being honest about what worked and what didn't
What? So what? Now what?
A structure for reflective writing
Evidence in evaluation
Test results, feedback and comparisons with the brief
Weak evaluation
"I think it went well" with no evidence or reason

Quick check

1. Which is the strongest evaluation?

  1. I think it went well.
  2. My poster was good.
  3. In testing, 8 of 10 users understood the message in 5 seconds, but younger users found the font hard to read, so I'd increase its size.
  4. I liked it.
Show answer
C: In testing, 8 of 10 users understood the message in 5 seconds, but younger users found the font hard to read, so I'd increase its size.. Evidence, a weakness and an improvement.

2. What is creative bravery?

  1. Never changing your idea
  2. Taking risks and honestly admitting what didn't work
  3. Working alone
  4. Using bright colours
Show answer
B: Taking risks and honestly admitting what didn't work. Risk-taking plus honest reflection.

3. When should you evaluate?

  1. Only at the very end
  2. Throughout the project
  3. Never
  4. Only if the idea fails
Show answer
B: Throughout the project. Evaluate as you go.
