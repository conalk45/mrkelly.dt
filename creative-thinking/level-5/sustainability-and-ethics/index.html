The circular economy

In a linear economy we take, make and throw away. In a circular economy products are designed so materials are kept in use: designed out waste, products reused, repaired and remade, and materials recycled or returned safely to nature.

  • Design for durability, repair and easy disassembly.
  • Use recycled, renewable or locally sourced materials.
  • Offer take-back, rental or refill schemes.
  • Make on demand to avoid unsold stock.

Ethical design

  • Fair pay and safe working conditions for makers.
  • Honest advertising: no false claims or "greenwashing".
  • Respect for people's privacy and wellbeing.
  • Inclusive and accessible to all.

Greenwashing is claiming a product is more environmentally friendly than it really is.

Key terms

Circular economy
Keeping materials in use: reduce, reuse, repair, remake, recycle
Linear economy
Take, make, throw away
Greenwashing
Claiming something is greener than it really is
Design for disassembly
Making products easy to take apart to repair or recycle
Make on demand
Producing only what has been ordered, to avoid waste

Quick check

1. Which is part of a circular economy?

  1. Throwing away old clothes
  2. A take-back scheme that remakes old products
  3. Buying more
  4. Single-use packaging
Show answer
B: A take-back scheme that remakes old products. Materials stay in use.

2. A brand claims "100% eco" with no evidence. This is…

  1. Biomimicry
  2. Greenwashing
  3. Upcycling
  4. Prototyping
Show answer
B: Greenwashing. Misleading green claims.

3. Which design choice helps repair?

  1. Glueing everything shut
  2. Using standard screws so parts can be replaced
  3. Using mixed materials fused together
  4. Hiding all fixings
Show answer
B: Using standard screws so parts can be replaced. Easy disassembly helps repair.
