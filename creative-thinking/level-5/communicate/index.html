Communicating ideas

Your final idea needs to be understood quickly by someone who hasn't seen your process: the client, the user or an assessor. Choose the format that suits the brief:

  • Presentation boards or posters
  • Pitch or slideshow
  • Short film or animation
  • Mock-ups: a model, a product, a fashion piece, a web page
  • Storyboards for campaigns and experiences

Making it clear and creative

  • Visual hierarchy: the most important thing is biggest or boldest; viewers know where to look first.
  • Explain the "why": show the problem, who it's for and how your idea helps.
  • Show it in use: people using it, the space with people in it, the ad on a real bus stop.
  • Keep text short; use images, icons and diagrams.
  • Be creative with the format: an unexpected way of presenting can make your idea memorable.

Expected outcomes

Say what you expect your idea to achieve: who will benefit and how you'd know it worked (e.g. "More people would use the space after school").

What catches people out

  • Final boards full of text. Use images of the idea in use, a clear title and short labels. Communicate element

Key terms

Visual hierarchyMaking the most important information stand out first
PitchA short persuasive presentation of your idea
Mock-upA realistic model or visual showing what the final idea would look like
Expected outcomeWhat you expect your idea to achieve and for whom
Show it in usePresent the idea being used by real people in a real setting

Quick check

1. What is visual hierarchy?

  1. Using lots of different bright colours
  2. Using only one font throughout the page
  3. Putting all the text into a bulleted list
  4. Making the most important thing stand out first
Show answer
D: Making the most important thing stand out first. It guides the viewer's eye.

2. Which makes a presentation board clearer?

  1. Short text with images showing the idea in use
  2. Long paragraphs explaining every detail
  3. Lots of small images with no captions
  4. No title, so the images speak for themselves
Show answer
A: Short text with images showing the idea in use. Images and short text communicate faster.
