Notes

Designing for people

User-centred design starts with the people who will use your idea. Empathy means understanding how they think and feel, not just what they need.

Tools for empathy

  • Persona: a made-up but realistic profile of a typical user (name, age, needs, frustrations, goals) based on your research.
  • Empathy map: what the user says, thinks, does and feels.
  • User journey: each step the user takes, with highs and lows marked.
  • "How might we…?" question: turns a problem into a challenge, e.g. "How might we make a hospital waiting room feel calm for children?"

Inclusive design

Think about people who are often left out: disabled people, older people, people with less money, people who speak other languages. Designing for them often makes things better for everyone (e.g. dropped kerbs help wheelchairs, prams and suitcases).

Quick check

1. What are the four parts of an empathy map?

  1. Who, what, where, when
  2. Says, thinks, does, feels
  3. Cost, size, colour, shape
  4. Research, concept, test, evaluate
Show answer
B: Says, thinks, does, feels. Says, thinks, does, feels.

2. A persona should be based on…

  1. Your imagination only
  2. Your research about real users
  3. A celebrity
  4. Your best friend
Show answer
B: Your research about real users. Personas summarise real research.

3. Which is a good "How might we" question?

  1. Make a poster.
  2. How might we help new pupils find their way round school on day one?
  3. Is the school big?
  4. Design a logo.
Show answer
B: How might we help new pupils find their way round school on day one?. It names the user and the challenge.

4. Why design inclusively?

  1. It is always cheaper
  2. It often makes things better for everyone
  3. It uses fewer colours
  4. It isn't useful
Show answer
B: It often makes things better for everyone. e.g. dropped kerbs help everyone.
