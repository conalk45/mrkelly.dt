SCAMPER

Take an existing product or idea and ask:

  • Substitute: what could be swapped (material, user, place)?
  • Combine: what could be joined together?
  • Adapt: what could be borrowed from somewhere else?
  • Modify (magnify/minimise): what could be bigger, smaller, changed?
  • Put to another use: could it be used differently?
  • Eliminate: what could be removed?
  • Reverse/rearrange: what if you did it backwards or in a different order?

Lateral thinking

Lateral thinking means approaching a problem from unexpected angles instead of the obvious step-by-step route. Ask "What if…?", look at how other industries or nature solve similar problems (biomimicry), and question the rules everyone assumes.

Six thinking hats L6

Look at an idea in six ways: white (facts), red (feelings), black (risks), yellow (benefits), green (new ideas), blue (managing the thinking). Useful for evaluating and improving concepts as a group.

Quick check

1. In SCAMPER, what does the E stand for?

  1. Evaluate
  2. Eliminate
  3. Explore
  4. Expand
Show answer
B: Eliminate. Eliminate: what could be removed?

2. Which technique asks you to sketch eight ideas in eight minutes?

  1. Mind map
  2. SCAMPER
  3. Crazy 8s
  4. Six hats
Show answer
C: Crazy 8s. Crazy 8s forces fast, varied ideas.

3. Velcro was inspired by burrs sticking to a dog. This is an example of…

  1. Reverse brainstorming
  2. Converging
  3. A survey
  4. Biomimicry
Show answer
D: Biomimicry. Copying nature is biomimicry.

4. What is the first rule of brainstorming?

  1. No judging
  2. Only good ideas
  3. Work alone
  4. Pick one quickly
Show answer
A: No judging. Judging stops ideas flowing.

5. Which thinking hat looks at the risks?

  1. White
  2. Black
  3. Yellow
  4. Green
Show answer
B: Black. Black hat is caution.
