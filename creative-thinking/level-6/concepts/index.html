Concepts

From research to ideas

Your concepts should grow out of your research. Point to the finding that sparked each idea: "Because users said the waiting room feels cold and clinical, this concept uses…"

Aim for quantity first, quality later. Lots of ideas, including silly ones, make it more likely you'll find an original one.

Diverge, then converge

  • Diverge: go wide and generate as many different ideas as you can, without judging them.
  • Converge: then narrow down, choosing the most promising using your research and the brief.

Use techniques from the Idea generation topic: mind maps, SCAMPER, random words, Crazy 8s, "worst possible idea".

Choosing between ideas

  • Check each idea against the brief and the user's needs.
  • Use a simple comparison table or score each idea against criteria (originality, does it solve the problem, is it possible?).
  • Get feedback from users or classmates.
  • Record why you chose one idea and dropped others: that reasoning is evidence of critical thinking.

Level 6: range and originality L6

At Level 6 your concepts should show a wider range, including unexpected directions, and you should combine ideas from different sources: another industry, nature, history or another culture.

What catches people out

  • One idea developed from the start. Generate a wide range, including unexpected directions, then choose. Concepts element

Quick check

1. What should concepts be based on?

  1. Whatever looks coolest
  2. The cheapest option
  3. The teacher's idea
  4. The research you've done
Show answer
D: The research you've done. Concepts must grow out of research.

2. What does "diverge" mean in idea generation?

  1. Generate lots of different ideas
  2. Pick one idea quickly and develop it
  3. Throw away ideas that seem odd
  4. Copy an existing product closely
Show answer
A: Generate lots of different ideas. Diverge first, converge later.

3. Why record why you rejected some ideas?

  1. To fill pages
  2. It shows your critical thinking
  3. It's required by law
  4. It makes the folder heavier
Show answer
B: It shows your critical thinking. Your reasoning is evidence.

4. When should you judge ideas?

  1. While you are still generating them
  2. Never: every idea is equally good
  3. After generating lots of them
  4. Before doing any research
Show answer
C: After generating lots of them. Judging too early kills ideas.
