Notes

Ergonomics

Improving the interaction between people and products so they are safe, comfortable and easy to use.

  • Anthropometrics: human body measurements (height, reach, grip, hand width).
  • Physiology: strength, effort, movement, posture, fatigue and strain.
  • Psychology: how the product looks, feels, sounds, smells and tastes affects how a user perceives and uses it.

Link specific body parts to specific parts of the product: "the handle diameter suits the grip of a 50th percentile adult hand".

Anthropometric data and percentiles

Body sizes follow a bell curve. Designers usually design for the 5th–95th percentile range (90% of users).

  • Reach: design for the 5th percentile so small users can reach (shelves, emergency stop buttons).
  • Clearance: design for the 95th percentile so large users fit (doorways, leg room).
  • Comfort/average: 50th percentile, or make it adjustable.

UK standing height (mm): men 5th 1625 / 50th 1740 / 95th 1855; women 1505 / 1610 / 1710.

Safety

  • Rounded edges and corners; guards over moving parts and finger traps; stable wide bases.
  • Non-slip textures and secure grips; electrical insulation; non-toxic materials and finishes.
  • Warning labels where a risk cannot be removed; clear instructions; emergency stops.

Ensuring safety (Higher)

  • Test design proposals and prototypes, including test rigs simulating extended use.
  • Meet British Standards (BSI) and CE marking requirements. CE shows the maker has checked the product meets European safety, health and environmental requirements.
  • Consider safety at every stage: manufacture (training, PPE, machine maintenance), use (clear operation, warnings, misuse) and end of life (safe disassembly, sealed toxic parts).

Key terms

Anthropometrics
The study of human body measurements used to size products.
Physiology
Strength, effort, movement and strain required to use a product.
Psychology (ergonomics)
How a product's look, feel, sound, smell or taste affects the user.
Percentile for reach
5th percentile, so smaller users can reach.
Percentile for clearance
95th percentile, so larger users fit through or under.
CE marking
Shows the product meets European safety, health and environmental requirements.
BSI
British Standards Institution: sets and regulates standards for products.

Quick check

1. Which percentile should be used for the height of a doorway?

  1. 5th
  2. 50th
  3. 95th
  4. 1st
Show answer
C: 95th. Clearance must suit the largest users.

2. Which percentile is used to position an emergency stop button?

  1. 5th
  2. 50th
  3. 95th
  4. 99th
Show answer
A: 5th. If the smallest users can reach it, everyone else can.

3. "The force needed to squeeze the trigger is low so the user does not tire" is an example of…

  1. Anthropometrics
  2. Physiology
  3. Psychology
  4. Aesthetics
Show answer
B: Physiology. Effort and fatigue are physiology.

4. A bright red, clearly labelled off switch reassures the user. This is…

  1. Psychology
  2. Anthropometrics
  3. Physiology
  4. Function only
Show answer
A: Psychology. How the product looks and the confidence it gives is psychology.

5. Which feature mainly improves safety?

  1. Brand logo
  2. Guard over moving blades
  3. Glossy finish
  4. Secondary storage
Show answer
B: Guard over moving blades. Guards prevent finger traps and contact with blades.

6. What does CE marking show?

  1. The product is patented
  2. It meets European safety, health and environmental requirements
  3. It was made in Europe
  4. It is recyclable
Show answer
B: It meets European safety, health and environmental requirements. CE is a conformity mark, not a country of origin.

Exam-style questions

1. Describe how ergonomics has influenced the design of a product such as a cross trainer. (4 marks)

Show marking guidance
  • Anthropometrics: handle diameter suits grip of users; footrest length/width; reach to handles; adjustability (body part + product part + impact = 1)
  • Physiology: force needed to operate is variable; posture is upright and comfortable
  • Psychology: looks simple and safe to operate; sounds smooth; feels robust
  • Max 3 marks for any one area at Higher when all three are required

2. Describe how the design of a product has been influenced by safety issues. (4 marks)

Show marking guidance
  • Stable wide base / secured to ground
  • Covers over moving parts to prevent finger traps
  • Non-slip pattern on foot rests
  • Rubber grips for secure hold
  • Electrical insulation / emergency stop
  • 1 mark per valid description
