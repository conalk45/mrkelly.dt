Why use idea-generation techniques

Generating a range of original ideas is hard. Techniques give a starting point, help overcome design block, encourage creative and diverse thinking, and give structure.

Brainstorming

  • State the problem clearly from the start.
  • Work alone or in a small group; consider who to invite for a range of opinions.
  • Set a time limit. Record every idea, however unrealistic.
  • Don't criticise ideas; build on each other's.
  • Record as lists, diagrams or mind maps.

Brainstorming: Higher detail HIGHER

  • Planning: identify the purpose, consider group size, invite suitable people.
  • Conducting: rephrase the question, warm up, set rules and a time limit, keep momentum, record every thought.
  • Summary: discuss unusual ideas, discard the weakest, identify the most promising.

Morphological analysis

A systematic method using a matrix. The problem is broken into variables (e.g. material, number of legs, height, shape of top). Options for each are listed in columns, then one option from each column is selected, randomly or systematically, to produce different combinations.

Analogy, lifestyle and mood boards HIGHER

  • Analogy: take an idea from one context and use it in another. Nike based its Shox cushioning on Formula One suspension. Stages: identify stimuli, gather images/information, select what to transfer or adapt.
  • Lifestyle board: images representing the typical user's life (home, food, clothes, hobbies, music) so the designer understands the target market.
  • Mood board: a collage around a mood or theme to inspire ideas and set an aesthetic direction (colours, patterns, textures, shapes).

Developing ideas

  • Explore: identify problems to solve and investigate alternatives for function, aesthetics, ergonomics and manufacture.
  • Synthesis: combine the best elements of different ideas into a stronger one.
  • Refine: add the detail needed to manufacture: final sizes, materials, construction and assembly.
  • Justify decisions with annotation; use arrows, ticks and crosses so the design thinking is easy to follow.

What catches people out

  • Descriptions of brainstorming and morphological analysis too basic. Up to 3 marks per technique: planning, conducting, summary. Higher 2022

Quick check

1. How does morphological analysis improve the breadth of ideas?

  1. It removes weak ideas
  2. It creates many unique combinations of variables
  3. It tests ideas with users
  4. It records ideas as sketches
Show answer
B: It creates many unique combinations of variables. Combining different options from each column produces many alternatives.

2. Which is a rule of brainstorming?

  1. Only feasible ideas are recorded
  2. Ideas are judged as they appear
  3. Every idea is recorded without criticism
  4. It must be done alone
Show answer
C: Every idea is recorded without criticism. Judging ideas too early stops creative thinking.

3. Nike basing a trainer's cushioning on Formula One suspension is an example of…

  1. Mood board
  2. Analogy
  3. Morphological analysis
  4. Synthesis
Show answer
B: Analogy. Analogy transfers an idea from one context into another.

4. A collage that shows where the user lives, what they wear and their hobbies is a…

  1. Mood board
  2. Lifestyle board
  3. Morphological matrix
  4. Working drawing
Show answer
B: Lifestyle board. Lifestyle boards help the designer understand the target user.

5. Combining the best features of several initial ideas is called…

  1. Refinement
  2. Synthesis
  3. Evaluation
  4. Analysis
Show answer
B: Synthesis. Synthesis brings together strengths from different ideas.

Exam-style questions

1. Explain one way in which brainstorming can encourage creative thoughts. (1 mark)

Show marking guidance
  • Done as a group so it brings several people's ideas together
  • People build on each other's ideas
  • Time limit means less chance to overthink or self-censor

2. Describe two idea-generation techniques that could be used. (You may use sketches.) (4 marks)

Show marking guidance
  • Up to 3 marks per technique; no marks for naming only
  • Brainstorming: group activity, problem stated simply, all ideas welcomed, no criticism, build on others' ideas
  • Morphological analysis: product divided into parameters, options listed for each, one from each column selected to describe a possible design
  • Analogy / mood or lifestyle board described with stages also acceptable
