Impact & sustainability

Notes

Impact on society and the workforce

  • Machines, CAD/CAM and automation produce more, faster and more accurately than manual methods.
  • Workforce: fewer people needed; jobs lost to machines and to cheaper labour abroad; demand for new skills (CAD operators, machine supervisors and maintenance) and fewer traditional craftspeople.
  • Supply of products: greater choice, cheaper products, a consumer and throwaway society.
  • Companies must invest heavily in machines, maintenance and training.

Impact on the environment

  • Uses up the earth's resources for materials and energy.
  • Generates waste.
  • Pollution from manufacturing, transport and disposal of products and packaging, contributing to global warming.
  • Positive: new technologies can be more energy efficient and produce less waste (e.g. CAM nesting, 3D printing).

Energy: manufacturing uses large amounts of energy, much still from fossil fuels, adding to emissions. New technologies can reduce this: more efficient machines, renewable energy in factories, products that use less energy in use (LED lighting, A-rated appliances), and lighter products that need less fuel to transport.

Supporting sustainability

Sustainability means using resources responsibly so future generations can still meet their needs. Clean manufacturing includes sourcing materials sustainably and locally, minimising transport, energy use and waste.

The 6 Rs: Reduce (less material), Rethink (a less damaging solution), Refuse (unnecessary items, e.g. excess packaging), Recycle (use recycled or recyclable materials), Reuse (another use for the product or its parts), Repair (easy to fix, extending life).

Circular economy: nothing goes to waste; products are designed to be reused, repaired, rented or bought back.

Higher detail

  • Design for recyclability: single materials, labelled parts, easy disassembly (screws not adhesives).
  • Design for reuse: refillable or repurposable products and parts.
  • Efficiency: less material, efficient processes and packaging, energy-efficient products.
  • Materials: plastics from fossil fuels; hardwoods and deforestation; composites hard to recycle; bio-composites and recycled materials reduce impact.
  • Investigating a product's sustainability: materials, manufacture, transport, use (energy), life expectancy, disposal.

Key terms

Sustainability
Using resources responsibly so future generations can still meet their needs.
The 6 Rs
Reduce, Rethink, Refuse, Recycle, Reuse, Repair.
Clean manufacturing
Sustainable/local materials, less transport, less energy, less waste.
Impact of automation on workforce
Fewer jobs and loss of traditional skills; demand for CAD and maintenance skills.
Circular economy
Nothing goes to waste; products are reused, repaired or returned.
Design for recyclability
Single materials, labelled parts, easy disassembly.

Quick check

1. Which is a negative impact of automation on the workforce?

  1. More CAD jobs
  2. Loss of traditional craft skills and fewer jobs
  3. Safer working
  4. Higher accuracy
Show answer
B: Loss of traditional craft skills and fewer jobs. Machines replace manual labour.

2. Designing a product to be fixed easily, extending its life, is which R?

  1. Reduce
  2. Refuse
  3. Repair
  4. Rethink
Show answer
C: Repair. Repair keeps products in use.

3. Which design decision best supports recycling?

  1. Gluing different plastics together
  2. Using one material and labelling it
  3. Adding chrome plating
  4. Using composites
Show answer
B: Using one material and labelling it. Single labelled materials are easy to separate.

4. Sourcing materials locally mainly reduces…

  1. Transport pollution
  2. Product aesthetics
  3. Strength
  4. Branding
Show answer
A: Transport pollution. Shorter distances mean less fuel.

5. Why are composites a concern for sustainability?

  1. They are too light
  2. They are difficult to separate and recycle
  3. They rust
  4. They are always toxic
Show answer
B: They are difficult to separate and recycle. Bonded fibres and resin are hard to recover.

Exam-style questions

1. Describe the impact that design and manufacturing technologies have had on the workforce. (2 marks)

Show marking guidance
  • Fewer workers needed as machines replace manual labour (1)
  • Change in skills: demand for CAD operators/maintenance; loss of traditional skills (1)

2. Describe how a designer could reduce the impact of a product on the environment. (3 marks)

Show marking guidance
  • Use recycled or recyclable materials, labelled (1)
  • Design for disassembly with screws so parts can be repaired/recycled (1)
  • Reduce material and packaging; source locally; energy-efficient in use (1)

3. Describe how consideration of the environment has impacted on the design, manufacture and use of a product such as a scooter. (4 marks)

Show marking guidance
  • Materials: recyclable aluminium/plastics, labelled (1)
  • Manufacture: efficient processes, low waste, local supply (1)
  • Use: long life, replaceable wheels/parts, no energy needed (1)
  • Disposal: easy to dismantle and recycle (1)
