Joining, assembly & finishing

10 cards · 7 quiz · 2 exam

Design & Manufacture · Higher · mrkellydt.co.uk

Wood joints

  • Frame joints: mortise and tenon (strong; tables, chairs, frames), bridle (strong corner joint for frames), halving (simple frames), dowel (replaces traditional joints in factory furniture), lap.
  • Rub joint: two planed edges glued together side by side to make a wide board (table tops, panels).
  • Carcase joints: housing (bookshelves, cabinets; stopped housing looks neater), rebate (fitting back panels), butt (quick but weak), dovetail (drawers, decorative), mitre (hides end grain).

Fixings and adhesives

  • Screws: countersunk (flush), round head (head can stand proud), chipboard screws. Drill a pilot hole, then a clearance hole in the top piece, then countersink.
  • Nails and pins: panel pins hold glued joints while they set; oval brads less likely to split wood; punch below surface with a nail punch.
  • Nuts and bolts: strong and removable.
  • Pop rivets: join thin sheet from one side.
  • Welding: permanent, strong joint in metal.
  • PVA: general woodworking, non-toxic, about an hour to set. Epoxy resin: two parts, joins different materials (metal, plastic, wood).
  • Knock-down fittings: non-permanent, for flat-pack furniture (cam locks, corner blocks, cross dowels). Allow self-assembly, easy storage and transport, avoid complex joints.

Features that aid assembly: bosses, location pins, ribs, symmetrical parts, labelling. Issues: volume, materials, function, safety, aesthetics, life expectancy, environment.

Identifying assembly methods: take the product apart and reassemble it; look for visible screws, rivet heads, weld beads, glue lines, moulded clips and snap-fit features.

Surface finishing

  • Wood: sand with the grain through finer grades, then varnish (hard, protective), oil (natural look, food safe on some), stain (changes colour), wax (sheen), paint.
  • Metal: file (cross then draw filing), emery cloth, wet and dry paper, polish; then lacquer, paint or dip-coat to stop corrosion.
  • Plastic (acrylic edges): file, draw file, wet and dry, then polish.
  • Dip coating: heat metal in an oven, dip into fluidised plastic powder; gives a smooth, coloured, protective, comfortable finish.
  • Poor finishes come from dust, runs, brush marks, skipping grades, or not removing oil and dirt.

Commercial coatings (Higher) HIGHER

  • Chrome plating: electroplated chromium layer. Mirror finish, corrosion resistant, hard, easy to clean (taps, car trim).
  • Galvanising: steel dipped in molten zinc. Corrosion resistant. Identified by a "spangled" mottled finish (fences, railings, nails).
  • Plastic dip coating: smooth coloured plastic coat. Protects, adds grip and comfort (tool handles, dish racks).

What catches people out

  • Factors affecting the choice of assembly method poorly explained. Volume, materials, permanent or not, maintenance, recycling, flat-pack, safety. Higher 2023

Quick check

1. Which joint is best for fixing shelves into the sides of a bookcase?

  1. Halving
  2. Housing
  3. Mortise and tenon
  4. Lap
Show answer
B: Housing. Housings support the shelf along its width.

2. Why are knock-down fittings used in flat-pack furniture?

  1. They are permanent
  2. Product can be packed flat and assembled at home
  3. They are stronger than any joint
  4. They hide all screws
Show answer
B: Product can be packed flat and assembled at home. Easy storage, transport and self-assembly.

3. Which adhesive would join aluminium to acrylic?

  1. PVA
  2. Epoxy resin
  3. Wood glue
  4. Glue stick
Show answer
B: Epoxy resin. Epoxy bonds dissimilar non-porous materials.

4. Before varnishing wood you should…

  1. Sand against the grain
  2. Sand with the grain through finer grades and remove dust
  3. Apply oil first
  4. Wet the surface
Show answer
B: Sand with the grain through finer grades and remove dust. Preparation gives a quality finish.

5. What is the purpose of a pilot hole for a screw?

  1. To let the head sit flush
  2. To guide the screw and stop the wood splitting
  3. To make the screw removable
  4. To hold glue
Show answer
B: To guide the screw and stop the wood splitting. A clearance hole lets the top piece pull tight; countersinking sets the head flush.

6. A galvanised fence post can be identified by…

  1. A mirror finish
  2. A spangled, mottled surface
  3. A smooth coloured plastic coat
  4. Visible layer lines
Show answer
B: A spangled, mottled surface. Zinc crystals form spangles.

7. Which method joins car body panels quickly on an automated line?

  1. Arc welding by hand
  2. Spot welding
  3. Dovetail joints
  4. Thermal bonding
Show answer
B: Spot welding. Robots spot weld overlapping sheet steel.

Exam-style questions

1. Describe how a mild steel part would be prepared and finished by dip coating. (3 marks)

Show marking guidance
  • Clean/degrease and remove burrs (1)
  • Heat the part in the oven (1)
  • Dip into the fluidiser of plastic powder, which melts onto the hot metal (1)
  • Allow to cool / reheat to smooth (1)

2. Describe the benefits and drawbacks of using snap fits to assemble a product. (3 marks)

Show marking guidance
  • Benefit: fast assembly with no extra parts or tools, lower cost (1)
  • Benefit: features moulded in during injection moulding (1)
  • Drawback: may be difficult to dismantle for repair/recycling; clips can break (1)
