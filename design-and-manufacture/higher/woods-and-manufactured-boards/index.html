Notes

Softwoods and hardwoods

  • Softwoods come from coniferous trees (needles, evergreen). Grow quickly, often plantation grown and replaced, relatively cheap.
  • Hardwoods come from deciduous trees (broad leaves, shed in winter). Grow slowly, often not replaced, more expensive.

Woods you need to know

WoodPropertiesUses
Red pine / pine (soft)Strong, durable, glues and finishes well, easy to workFloorboards, joists, roof trusses, toys
Spruce (soft)Strong, resists splitting, easy to workGeneral indoor work, furniture
Beech (hard)Very strong, heavy, can warp, turns and machines wellToys, wooden spoons, chairs, workbenches
Ash (hard)Strong, tough, bends well, straight grainHockey sticks, bats, hammer handles
Oak (hard)Very durable, heavy, steam bends, glues and stains wellQuality furniture, flooring, barrels, boats
Mahogany (hard)Reasonably durable, glues and finishes well, easy to workQuality furniture, veneers, banisters

Manufactured boards

Made from waste wood. Cheaper than solid wood, come in large stable sheets that don't warp easily, often veneered or plastic-coated.

BoardPropertiesUses
PlywoodOdd number of veneers, grain at 90°; very strong, stable; indoor/outdoor gradesFurniture, cabinet backs, door fronts
Flexi-plyThin layers, bends easilyCurved furniture, skateboards
MDFFine fibres and glue; smooth flat surface, easy to shape, heavy, veneers wellFlat-pack furniture, skirting, pattern-making
ChipboardWood chips and glue; cheap, brittle, hard to screw into, not for outdoorsShelving, kitchen worktops (veneered)
HardboardThin, smooth one side, cheap, not strongWardrobe backs, drawer bottoms, clipboards
Blockboard (H)Strips of timber between veneers; strong, rigid, heavyShelving, doors, worktops

Veneered boards (Higher): a thin slice of quality hardwood (or a plastic laminate) is glued onto MDF or chipboard. The product looks like solid hardwood at a fraction of the cost and is stable, but chipped edges expose the core.

Key terms

Softwood source
Coniferous trees: evergreen, needle leaves, fast growing.
Hardwood source
Deciduous trees: broad leaves shed in winter, slow growing.
Beech
Hardwood: very strong, turns well. Toys, spoons, chairs.
Ash
Hardwood: tough, bends well. Sports bats, tool handles.
Oak
Hardwood: very durable. Quality and outdoor furniture, barrels.
Plywood
Odd number of veneers with grain at 90°: strong and stable.
MDF
Fine wood fibres and glue: smooth, flat, easy to machine. Flat-pack.
Advantage of manufactured boards
Cheap, large stable sheets that don't warp; uses waste wood.

Quick check

1. Which is a softwood?

  1. Beech
  2. Oak
  3. Spruce
  4. Mahogany
Show answer
C: Spruce. Spruce is coniferous.

2. Why does plywood have an odd number of layers with grain at 90°?

  1. To make it cheaper
  2. To reduce warping and add strength
  3. So it bends easily
  4. For colour
Show answer
B: To reduce warping and add strength. Alternating grain balances the sheet.

3. Which hardwood suits a cricket bat or hammer handle?

  1. Ash
  2. Mahogany
  3. MDF
  4. Spruce
Show answer
A: Ash. Ash is tough and shock-resistant.

4. Which board would you use for a curved chair back?

  1. Chipboard
  2. Hardboard
  3. Flexi-ply
  4. MDF
Show answer
C: Flexi-ply. Flexi-ply bends easily.

5. Which is a drawback of chipboard?

  1. Very expensive
  2. Brittle and hard to screw into
  3. Too flexible
  4. Only available in small widths
Show answer
B: Brittle and hard to screw into. Special chipboard screws are used for this reason.

Exam-style questions

1. Describe one benefit of using manufactured boards. (1 mark)

Show marking guidance
  • Available in large, stable sheets that don't warp
  • Cheaper than solid wood
  • Uses waste wood so more sustainable
  • Can be veneered to look like quality hardwood

2. A child's toy is made from beech. Explain why beech is suitable. (2 marks)

Show marking guidance
  • Strong/tough so it withstands rough play (1)
  • Close grain finishes smoothly / turns well so no splinters (1)
  • Non-toxic natural material safe for children (1)
