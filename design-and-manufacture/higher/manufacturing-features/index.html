Manufacturing features

6 cards · 4 quiz · 2 exam

Design & Manufacture · Higher · mrkellydt.co.uk

Spotting how something was made

  • Split lines: where two halves of a mould meet.
  • Flashing: thin excess material squeezed out where the mould halves meet.
  • Ejector pin marks: small circles where pins pushed the part out.
  • Injection point / sprue: where material entered the mould.
  • Draft angles: tapered sides for release.
  • Wall thickness: even (rotational moulding), thin and varying (vacuum forming).
  • Thinning of sheet material at corners (vacuum forming).
  • Shear marks on cut edges (piercing and blanking).
  • Surface finish: smooth (injection, die casting) or rough (sand casting).
  • Symbols and labels: recycling codes and material marks moulded in.

Strengthening and assembly features

  • Boss: a raised cylinder that takes a screw or thread; strengthens the area around the fixing.
  • Location pin: positions parts accurately for assembly and stops them moving.
  • Ribs and webs: thin walls that add strength and rigidity without thick sections, help material flow evenly into the mould, reduce distortion, and hold internal components (circuit boards, wires) in place.
  • Also moulded clips and threads remove the need for separate fixings.

What catches people out

  • Confused moulded features (bosses, location pins) with production aids (jigs, templates). Features are part of the moulding; jigs are separate tools. Higher 2024
  • Couldn't explain how features help accurate, efficient assembly. Location pins align, bosses take screws, ribs locate parts, asymmetric parts only fit one way. Higher 2022

Quick check

1. What is the main purpose of a boss on an injection-moulded casing?

  1. To decorate the outside of the moulded casing
  2. To take a screw and strengthen the fixing point
  3. To let trapped air escape from inside the mould
  4. To show the recycling code where it can be seen
Show answer
B: To take a screw and strengthen the fixing point. Bosses receive screws.

2. Why are ribs added to the inside of a plastic casing?

  1. To make the casing heavier so it feels solid and expensive
  2. To give a better grip on the outside of the casing
  3. Add rigidity without thick walls and hold internal parts
  4. To use up any extra plastic left in the mould
Show answer
C: Add rigidity without thick walls and hold internal parts. Thin ribs stiffen the part and locate components.

3. Thin excess plastic along the edge of a moulded part is called…

  1. Draft
  2. Rib
  3. Ejector pin mark
  4. Flashing
Show answer
D: Flashing. Flash forms where the mould halves meet.

4. Location pins mainly help with…

  1. Accurate assembly
  2. Corrosion resistance
  3. Aesthetics
  4. Recycling
Show answer
A: Accurate assembly. They align parts.

Exam-style questions

1. Explain the purpose of location pins, bosses and ribs/webs. (4 marks)

Show marking guidance
  • Location pins: position parts accurately; stop movement between parts
  • Bosses: provide a space for a screw/thread; add strength at the fixing
  • Ribs/webs: strength and rigidity; even material flow; locate internal components
  • 1 mark per valid explanation

2. Describe the features of a product that identify it was injection moulded. (3 marks)

Show marking guidance
  • Ejector pin marks (1)
  • Split line with possible flashing (1)
  • Injection point / sprue mark (1)
  • Draft angles, bosses and ribs, high-quality finish (1)
