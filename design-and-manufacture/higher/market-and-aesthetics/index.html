Market & aesthetics

Higher Design & Manufacture revision notes, 8 key terms, practice questions with answers and a printable worksheet.

Notes

Target market

The group of people a product is aimed at (age, gender, income, lifestyle). It influences function, performance, aesthetics, price, user interface and how the product is promoted.

Technology push and market pull

  • Technology push: new technology, materials or processes from R&D drive new products (many electronic products, e.g. mobile phones).
  • Market pull: consumer demand drives new or improved products, often after a competitor launches something.

Marketing techniques to influence sales

  • Pricing strategies: launch offers, discounts and sales; premium pricing to suggest quality; price matching competitors.
  • Advertising and promotion: TV, online and social media adverts, sponsorship, celebrity endorsement, special offers such as buy-one-get-one-free.
  • Product placement: products shown in films, TV shows and games, or placed in prominent positions in shops.
  • Packaging and point of sale: eye-catching packaging and in-store displays.

Branding

A brand is everything people associate with a company or product: its name, logo, reputation, promise and customer service.

  • Benefits to the company: recognition, customer loyalty and repeat sales, can charge higher prices, new products launch more easily.
  • Benefits to the consumer: trust and confidence in quality and reliability, easy to recognise.
  • Benefits to the retailer: predictable sales and customers who come looking for the brand.

Higher market topics

  • Brand loyalty levels: recognition → preference → insistence.
  • Niche marketing: targeting a small segment with specific needs. Less competition, loyal customers who may pay more; but higher unit cost, reliance on one product, vulnerable to market change.
  • Product life cycle: introduction (build awareness) → growth (build preference, market share) → maturity (defend market share, competitors appear) → decline (sales fall; rejuvenate with new features or uses).
  • Economics: development, tooling, material and labour costs. Economies of scale reduce unit cost in high volumes.
  • Fashion, trends and style: affect aesthetics, sales and how long a product stays desirable.
  • Failed launches: poor market research, budget spent before launch, slow distribution, quality or price problems. A well-handled recall can protect reputation.

Aesthetics

Our response to how a product looks and feels. First impressions influence whether people buy.

Aesthetic elements: shape and form, proportion, colour, texture, pattern, line, balance, contrast and harmony, materials.

Influences: target market, where it will be used, fashion and style, materials and processes, and (Higher) look, feel, smell, sound and taste.

Key terms

Technology push
New technology or materials from R&D drive the creation of new products.
Market pull
Consumer demand drives new or improved products.
Marketing techniques
Pricing strategies, advertising and promotion, product placement, packaging and displays.
Benefit of branding to consumer
Trust and confidence in quality; recognisable products.
Benefit of branding to company
Loyalty, higher prices, easier launches, market share.
Product life cycle stages
Introduction, growth, maturity, decline.
Niche marketing
Targeting a small market segment with specific needs.
Aesthetic elements
Form, proportion, colour, texture, pattern, contrast, harmony, materials.

Quick check

1. A company launches a folding phone because a new flexible screen has been developed. This is…

  1. Market pull
  2. Technology push
  3. Niche marketing
  4. Branding
Show answer
B: Technology push. The technology drove the product.

2. A brand of trainers appears on a character in a popular film. This marketing technique is…

  1. Product placement
  2. Technology push
  3. Niche marketing
  4. Planned obsolescence
Show answer
A: Product placement. Showing the product within films, TV or games is product placement.

3. Which is a benefit of a strong brand to the consumer?

  1. Lower quality
  2. Confidence in quality and reliability
  3. More choice of logos
  4. Free maintenance
Show answer
B: Confidence in quality and reliability. A trusted brand reassures buyers.

4. At which life-cycle stage do competitors appear and the company focuses on defending market share?

  1. Introduction
  2. Growth
  3. Maturity
  4. Decline
Show answer
C: Maturity. Sales growth slows and rivals arrive at maturity.

5. Which is a disadvantage of niche marketing?

  1. Less competition
  2. Loyal customers
  3. Higher cost due to low volumes
  4. Clear focus
Show answer
C: Higher cost due to low volumes. Small volumes mean less economy of scale.

6. "I'll only buy this brand" describes…

  1. Brand recognition
  2. Brand preference
  3. Brand insistence
  4. Brand identity
Show answer
C: Brand insistence. Insistence is the strongest level of loyalty.

Exam-style questions

1. Describe how the needs of the target market have influenced the design of a product of your choice. (2 marks)

Show marking guidance
  • Link a feature to a user need, e.g. bright colours and rounded forms for young children (1)
  • Tough/durable materials for site workers; large controls for older users (1)

2. Explain two benefits of a strong brand image. (2 marks)

Show marking guidance
  • Consumers trust the quality, giving brand loyalty and repeat sales (1)
  • Company can charge higher prices / launch new products more easily (1)

3. Describe the stages of the product life cycle and how a company may encourage sales at each stage. (4 marks)

Show marking guidance
  • Introduction: advertising/launch offers to build awareness (1)
  • Growth: build brand preference, expand distribution (1)
  • Maturity: price promotions, new colours to defend market share (1)
  • Decline: add new features, find new uses or discount to clear stock (1)
