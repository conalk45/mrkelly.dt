Choosing a system (Higher): expected demand and sales, unit cost, investment in tooling and machines, size and skill of the workforce, type of product and how often it changes, lead time.

Standard components

Parts made in huge numbers to an agreed standard (nuts, bolts, hinges, motors).

  • Benefits: cheap, consistent quality, reliable, easy to source and replace, save design and manufacturing time.
  • Drawbacks: depend on outside suppliers (delays, quality out of your control), need storage, may restrict the design.

Methods to improve production (Higher)

  • Automation / CNC: fewer errors, smaller workforce, more output, runs 24 hours.
  • CAD/CAM: flexible manufacturing, efficient, shared files, simulations, links to stock control.
  • Gantt charts: tasks against time; show concurrent tasks and critical tasks; manage workforce and deadlines.
  • Flow charts: sequence of events and decisions (terminator, process, decision, input).
  • Jigs (guide the tool) and fixtures (hold the part): accuracy, repeatability, no marking out, less skilled labour.
  • Just-in-time: materials and parts arrive exactly when needed. Saves warehouse cost and waste, shortens lead time; but depends on suppliers and can't cope with a sudden rise in orders.
  • Standard components: known quality, less assembly time, lower costs.

Key terms

Mass production
Large volumes of identical products; automated; low skill; low unit cost.
One-off production
A single unique product made by highly skilled workers.
Batch production
A set quantity made at once; production can switch between products.
Standard component
A part made in large numbers to an agreed standard (e.g. bolts, hinges).
Just-in-time
Supplies arrive exactly when needed, so no large stock is held.
Jig
Device that guides a tool for accurate, repeatable work.
Gantt chart
Bar chart showing tasks against time, including concurrent tasks.

Quick check

1. Which system has the highest set-up cost but the lowest cost per item?

  1. One-off
  2. Batch
  3. Mass
  4. Prototype
Show answer
C: Mass. Tooling is spread over huge numbers.

2. Which is a disadvantage of standard components for a manufacturer?

  1. Guaranteed quality
  2. Dependence on an outside supplier
  3. Easy to source
  4. Saves time
Show answer
B: Dependence on an outside supplier. Delays at the supplier stop production.

3. What is a drawback of just-in-time production?

  1. Large warehouses needed
  2. Cannot easily cope with a sudden increase in orders
  3. More waste
  4. Slower deliveries
Show answer
B: Cannot easily cope with a sudden increase in orders. No stock is held to absorb extra demand.

4. How does a jig improve production?

  1. Removes need for accurate tools
  2. Reduces marking out and improves accuracy and repeatability
  3. Increases waste
  4. Replaces all workers
Show answer
B: Reduces marking out and improves accuracy and repeatability. It locates or guides consistently.

Exam-style questions

1. Describe four ways in which a manufacturer could improve the efficiency of a batch production line. (8 marks)

Show marking guidance
  • Up to 2 marks per method described
  • Jigs/fixtures: no marking out, accurate repeat parts
  • CAD/CAM or CNC: automated, accurate, 24/7
  • Just-in-time: no stockholding, less waste
  • Standard components: no need to make parts, known quality
  • Gantt/flow charts: plan tasks, identify critical tasks, manage workforce
