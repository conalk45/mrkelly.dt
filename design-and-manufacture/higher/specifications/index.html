Specifications

5 cards · 5 quiz · 2 exam

Design & Manufacture · Higher · mrkellydt.co.uk

What a specification is

A list of everything the product must do, be and have. It sets clear boundaries for the designer and covers every design factor: function, aesthetics, market, performance, ergonomics and safety.

It is used to evaluate ideas throughout the design process, so designs keep meeting the client's and user's needs.

Writing good specification points HIGHER

At Higher the product is commercially manufactured, so points also cover volume, cost, materials and processes, standards and environment. Points must come from the brief and research, and be specific and measurable.

WeakStrong
Must hold a standard bulb.Must hold a 28 W bulb with a standard E14 screw fitting.
Must be easy to maintain.Must leave at least 20 mm clearance around the bulb so it is easy to replace.
Must be cheap.Must retail for no more than £20, with manufacturing cost about 30% of retail.
Must be environmentally friendly.All plastic parts must be labelled for recycling and separable without tools.

Three types of specification HIGHER

  • Product design specification (PDS): written before the product is designed. Gives direction to the design team, defines parameters, used to evaluate decisions. Contains function, aesthetics, performance, safety, ergonomics, cost, quantity, target market, where it will be used, environmental impact.
  • Performance specification: summarises the performance the product must achieve without saying how. Covers legislation, standards and regulations, sustainability, guarantee requirements, ease of use, durability.
  • Technical specification: written after the product is designed and tested. Gives the technical details needed to manufacture it: materials, processes, assembly methods, dimensions, weight, production costs, power supply, maintenance cycles, safety standards.

In the Higher assignment, use at most 3 points from the brief plus at least 8 from your research.

What catches people out

  • Gave either the purpose or an example of a specification, rarely both. One sentence of purpose, one specific example. Higher 2022

Key terms

Where does specification information come from?The design brief and the results of research.
Why is a specification important to a designer?It sets clear boundaries, gives direction, and is used to evaluate ideas against the client and user needs.
Product design specificationWritten before designing. Sets parameters and direction; includes function, aesthetics, cost, target market, quantity, environment of use.
Performance specificationStates the performance the product must meet without saying how: standards, legislation, sustainability, guarantees.

Quick check

1. Which is the strongest specification point?

  1. It must be safe
  2. It must look good
  3. It must fit a 40 mm diameter water bottle
  4. It must be durable
Show answer
C: It must fit a 40 mm diameter water bottle. It is specific and measurable, so ideas can be checked against it.

2. Which two sources provide information for a specification?

  1. Brief and research
  2. Sketches and models
  3. Working drawing and cutting list
  4. Marketing and retail
Show answer
A: Brief and research. Points come from the brief and from the findings of your research.

3. Which specification is produced AFTER the product has been designed and tested?

  1. Product design
  2. Performance
  3. Technical
  4. Design brief
Show answer
C: Technical. It records the technical detail needed to manufacture the finished design.

4. "Must comply with BS EN 71 toy safety standards" belongs mainly in which specification?

  1. Technical
  2. Performance
  3. Brief
  4. Cutting list
Show answer
B: Performance. Performance specs cover standards and regulations the product must meet.

5. Which is most likely in a technical specification?

  1. Target market profile
  2. Wall thickness of 2.5 mm in ABS
  3. Must appeal to teenagers
  4. Retail price under £20
Show answer
B: Wall thickness of 2.5 mm in ABS. Material and dimension detail needed for manufacture.

Exam-style questions

1. Explain why a specification is an important tool for a designer. (2 marks)

Show marking guidance
  • It clarifies what the solution must do/be/have (1)
  • Designer can evaluate ideas and decisions against it to make sure they meet the client/user needs (1)

2. Describe the purpose of a product design specification and a technical specification, giving an example of the information found in each. (4 marks)

Show marking guidance
  • PDS purpose: defines parameters, gives direction, used to evaluate decisions (1)
  • PDS example: target market, cost, quantity, dimensions, environment of use (1)
  • Technical purpose: gives technical detail required to manufacture, produced after design and testing (1)
  • Technical example: materials, processes, assembly, dimensions, production costs (1)
