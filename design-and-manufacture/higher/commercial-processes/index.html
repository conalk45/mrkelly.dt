Moulding and casting

ProcessUsesIdentifying features
Injection moulding (thermoplastic)Complex, accurate parts in high volumes: casings, toys, bottle crates, sunglasses framesEjector pin marks, injection point (sprue), split lines, flashing, draft angles, high-quality finish, bosses, ribs
Die casting (metal: aluminium, zinc)Accurate complex metal parts in high volumes: toy cars, pencil sharpeners, door handlesSame as injection moulding: ejector marks, split lines, flash, draft angles, high finish
Rotational moulding (thermoplastic)Large hollow forms: kayaks, bins, traffic barriers, play equipmentHollow, seamless, uniform wall thickness (thicker in corners), good finish, slight draft
Sand casting (metal)Low-volume solid or hollow metal parts: engine blocks, manhole covers, one-offsRough "orange peel" texture, draft angles, rounded corners and fillets, split line; often needs machining
Vacuum forming (thermoplastic sheet)Simple shallow forms: packaging trays, masks, bath tubs, chocolate insertsThin walls (thinner at corners and bottom), open tray shape, generous draft angles, rounded corners

Patterns for vacuum forming and sand casting

  • Draft (taper) on vertical faces so the pattern can be removed.
  • Radiused corners and edges: no sharp corners; material flows and the pattern releases.
  • Smooth surface: any marks transfer to the product.
  • Vacuum forming: pattern must be flat-backed, have no undercuts, and have vent holes in hollows so air is drawn out.
  • Sand casting: pattern is often split in two halves with location pins; made from a stable material like MDF or wood.
  • Sand casting: the pattern is slightly larger than the product to allow for shrinkage as the metal cools; a sprue and riser are added so metal can be poured in and air escape; cast parts are fettled (runners cut off and edges filed) afterwards.

More processes (Higher)

ProcessUsesIdentifying features
Blow mouldingSeamless hollow containers: bottlesSimple symmetrical shape, thin even walls, flash at base, no internal detail
Compression mouldingThermosets: plugs, pan handles, hard hatsAs other mouldings (flash, draft angles) but no undercuts
ExtrusionLong constant profiles in plastic or aluminium: window frames, curtain rails, pipes, tubesIdentical cross-section along the length, smooth finish
Drop forgingVery strong metal parts: spanners, tools, golf club headsSimple forms, thick sections, large radii and draft angles, flash line; process strengthens the metal
Piercing and blankingCutting sheet metal: washers, key blanks (blanking makes the shape, piercing makes holes)Shear marks, accurate, repeated identical parts
Press formingBends and deep draws in sheet metal: sinks, car panels, cansThin material, radiused corners, good finish, stress marks on deep draws
BendingTube, bar or sheet in wood, metal or plastic: chair frames, playground equipmentRadiused bends, few or no joins
Laser cuttingThin sheet wood/acrylic/metal: jewellery, lighting, signageVery accurate, scorch marks, crisp edges, flat sheet parts
3D printingLow-volume complex or bespoke parts, prototypes, prostheticsVisible layer lines, infill, marks from supports

Issues that influence process choice: volume and cost of production, material, function/performance/safety, complexity of form, accuracy and finish, method of assembly.

Identifying the process used: take the product apart and look at the component's complexity and form, its manufacturing features (split lines, ejector marks, flash, wall thickness, cross-section), the material, likely volume of production, and its function, quality and price.

Key terms

Injection moulding
High-volume complex thermoplastic parts. Ejector pin marks, split lines, flash.
Die casting
High-volume complex metal parts in aluminium/zinc. Same features as injection moulding.
Rotational moulding
Large hollow seamless plastic forms: kayaks, bins.
Sand casting
Low-volume metal parts with rough sandy texture.
Vacuum forming
Thermoplastic sheet pulled over a pattern: thin walls, draft angles.
Vent holes
Allow trapped air out of hollows in a vacuum-forming pattern.
Draft angle
A taper so the product or pattern releases from the mould.
Extrusion
Constant cross-section along the length: pipes, window frames.
Blow moulding
Hollow thin-walled containers like drinks bottles.
Drop forging
Hammering hot metal in a die; strong tools like spanners.
Blanking vs piercing
Blanking cuts out the shape; piercing cuts holes in it.
Press forming
Sheet metal pressed into a die: sinks, car panels.

Quick check

1. A plastic kayak is hollow, seamless and has an even wall thickness. Process?

  1. Injection moulding
  2. Vacuum forming
  3. Rotational moulding
  4. Die casting
Show answer
C: Rotational moulding. Rotational moulding makes large hollow forms.

2. Small circular marks on the inside of a plastic casing are evidence of…

  1. Ejector pins
  2. Sand grains
  3. Shear marks
  4. Layer lines
Show answer
A: Ejector pins. Ejector pins push the part out of the mould.

3. Why must a vacuum-forming pattern have draft angles?

  1. To add strength
  2. So it can be removed from the formed sheet
  3. To make the walls thicker
  4. To add colour
Show answer
B: So it can be removed from the formed sheet. A taper lets the pattern release.

4. A cast-iron manhole cover with a rough texture was most likely made by…

  1. Die casting
  2. Sand casting
  3. Injection moulding
  4. Press forming
Show answer
B: Sand casting. Sand leaves a rough surface.

5. Which process suits a toy car body in zinc alloy made in large numbers?

  1. Sand casting
  2. Die casting
  3. Vacuum forming
  4. Rotational moulding
Show answer
B: Die casting. High volume, accurate metal parts.

6. Why are there vent holes in vacuum-forming patterns?

  1. To save material
  2. So air trapped in hollows can be drawn out
  3. For decoration
  4. To cool the pattern
Show answer
B: So air trapped in hollows can be drawn out. Otherwise the plastic won't pull into hollows.

7. Plastic window frames with an identical profile along their length are made by…

  1. Blow moulding
  2. Extrusion
  3. Compression moulding
  4. Drop forging
Show answer
B: Extrusion. Extrusion produces continuous cross-sections.

8. Which process is used for thermosetting plastic plugs?

  1. Blow moulding
  2. Rotational moulding
  3. Compression moulding
  4. Vacuum forming
Show answer
C: Compression moulding. Thermosets are compression moulded.

Exam-style questions

1. Name an appropriate process to manufacture the plastic casing of a games controller and explain why it is suitable. (3 marks)

Show marking guidance
  • Injection moulding (1)
  • Produces complex forms with bosses/ribs accurately (1)
  • High-quality surface finish, no further finishing (1)
  • Economical for high volumes despite expensive moulds (1)

2. Describe two features of a vacuum-forming pattern and explain why each is needed. (4 marks)

Show marking guidance
  • Draft angles (1) so the pattern releases from the formed sheet (1)
  • Radiused corners (1) to prevent thinning/tearing and help release (1)
  • Vent holes (1) to let trapped air out of hollows (1)

3. Name three appropriate manufacturing processes used in the production of an outdoor cross trainer and explain why they are suitable. (6 marks)

Show marking guidance
  • Part + process (1) + suitability (1), max 2 per process
  • Extrusion of frame tubes: consistent cross-section
  • Bending of frame: strong form without joins
  • Injection moulding of footrests: complex, accurate, good finish
  • Piercing/blanking of plates and holes: accurate, repeatable
  • Die casting of fixings; dip/powder coating for corrosion resistance
