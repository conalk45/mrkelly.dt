Briefs, research & evaluation

5 cards · 5 quiz · 2 exam

Design & Manufacture · National 5 · mrkellydt.co.uk

Why we research

  • To inform the product design specification.
  • To clarify and inform decisions made while exploring and refining ideas.
  • Researching existing products identifies the factors that influence design, manufacture and marketing, and the materials, processes and assembly methods used.

Before gathering research, decide: what information is needed, how it will be gathered and how it will be displayed.

Primary and secondary research

  • Primary (you gather it): questionnaires, interviews, user trips, user trials, product comparisons, measuring critical sizes.
  • Secondary (someone else gathered it): books, journals, magazines, product reviews, internet.

Questionnaires: key stages

  1. Identify what information is required.
  2. Establish who, and how many people, will complete it.
  3. Select question types (open or closed).
  4. Trial the questionnaire.
  5. Distribute, collect and analyse the responses.

Keep it short: too many questions and people answer less carefully.

User trips: key stages

Usually carried out by a member of the design team, who knows what information to look for.

  1. Define the purpose of the user trip.
  2. Identify where the product will be used.
  3. Use the product and record strengths, weaknesses and opinions on its function, performance, ease of use and aesthetics.
  4. Record and analyse the results.

Choosing a research technique

TechniqueChoose it when you need…
Questionnaire / surveyOpinions from lots of people in the target market, quickly and anonymously; results easy to graph
User tripFirst-hand experience of how a product works in its real setting
User trialTo see how real users actually behave with the product
Measuring critical sizesExact dimensions of things the product must hold, fit or suit (anthropometrics)
Product comparisonTo spot strengths, weaknesses and gaps in the market among existing products
Books, internet, reviewsFacts, standards, material data and other users' opinions without testing yourself

Evaluating products

Methods: comparison to other products, user trials, comparison against the specification, questionnaires.

  • During development: test ideas, give feedback on decisions, identify areas to develop further.
  • Existing products: find out how and why they succeed, spot design opportunities, identify strengths and weaknesses.

What catches people out

  • Named an idea-generation technique when asked for a research technique. Research: user trip, user trial, questionnaire, comparison, reviews. Ideas: brainstorming, morphological analysis. N5 2019, 2025

Key terms

Primary researchInformation you gather yourself, e.g. questionnaires, user trips, user trials, product comparisons.
Secondary researchInformation gathered by someone else, e.g. books, journals, product reviews, websites.
Key stages of a questionnaireIdentify info needed → decide who/how many → choose question types → trial it → distribute, collect, analyse.
User tripA designer uses the product themselves, recording strengths and weaknesses of function, performance, ease of use and aesthetics.
Evaluation methods (N5)Comparison to other products, user trials, comparison against specification, questionnaires.

Quick check

1. Which of these is secondary research?

  1. A user trip
  2. A product review in a magazine
  3. A questionnaire you wrote
  4. Measuring critical sizes
Show answer
B: A product review in a magazine. Someone else gathered the information, so it is secondary.

2. What is the first key stage of a questionnaire?

  1. Trial the questionnaire
  2. Choose closed questions
  3. Identify what information is required
  4. Analyse the results
Show answer
C: Identify what information is required. You must know what you need to find out before deciding who to ask or what to ask.

3. Who normally carries out a user trip?

  1. A member of the target market
  2. A member of the design team
  3. A retailer
  4. A lawyer
Show answer
B: A member of the design team. The designer uses the product so they know exactly what aspects to record.

4. Why is a questionnaire trialled before it is distributed?

  1. To make it longer
  2. To find confusing or unhelpful questions before wasting responses
  3. So it counts as secondary research
  4. To choose the target market
Show answer
B: To find confusing or unhelpful questions before wasting responses. A trial run shows whether the questions produce useful answers.

5. Which is NOT a purpose of research during the design process?

  1. Inform the specification
  2. Clarify decisions during refinement
  3. Replace the need to evaluate
  4. Identify materials used in existing products
Show answer
C: Replace the need to evaluate. Research and evaluation work together; one does not replace the other.

Exam-style questions

1. A designer used a questionnaire to research existing toasters. Describe the key stages of a questionnaire. (3 marks)

Show marking guidance
  • Identify what information is required
  • Establish who and how many people will complete it
  • Select question types (open/closed)
  • Trial the questionnaire
  • Distribute, collect and analyse the results
  • 1 mark per stage, in a logical order

2. Describe how a user trip could be used to research an existing product. (3 marks)

Show marking guidance
  • Define the purpose of the user trip
  • Identify where the product is used and use it in that setting
  • Record strengths and weaknesses of function, performance, ease of use, aesthetics
  • Analyse results to inform the specification
