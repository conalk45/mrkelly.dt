Modelling

4 cards · 3 quiz · 1 exam

Design & Manufacture · National 5 · mrkellydt.co.uk

Why model

Models are used through the whole design process to:

  • Generate and explore: quick, low detail, communicate ideas that are hard to sketch.
  • Test and refine: check ergonomics, key dimensions, moving parts, how parts fit, manufacturing details. More detailed and accurate as refinement goes on.
  • Communicate: show the client the final appearance or function, gather market research.

Types of model

  • Sketch model: fast, cheap, any scrap material (card, foam, wire, clay). Explores ideas, checks proportion and stability.
  • Block model: solid, usually non-working, shows shape and form. Built up or carved from Styrofoam, MDF or clay. Often used to test ergonomics.
  • Scale model: for things too big or small to model full size (buildings, sets). Checks proportion against users/surroundings and assembly. Keep the scale consistent and include a reference point.
  • Computer-generated model: realistic, easy to change, test colours/materials, produce many drawings from one model, link to 3D printers and CNC machines, share digitally.

Key terms

Sketch modelQuick, cheap model from scrap materials to generate and explore ideas.
Block modelSolid, non-working model showing shape and form; often used to test ergonomics.
Scale modelModel at a different scale for objects too large or small to model full size.
Computer-generated model benefitEasy to change, realistic, can produce drawings and link to CAM/3D printing.

Quick check

1. Which model would a designer use to test the comfort of a handle grip?

  1. Scale model
  2. Block model
  3. Sketch model of a different product
  4. Exploded drawing
Show answer
B: Block model. Block models show the true form and are often used to test ergonomics.

2. An architect shows a new school building to the council. Which model?

  1. Sketch model
  2. Scale model
  3. Block model
  4. Computer-generated model
Show answer
B: Scale model. Too large to model full size.

3. Why are sketch models used early in the design process?

  1. They show the final finish
  2. They are quick and cheap to make and change
  3. They are fully working
  4. They are used for mass production
Show answer
B: They are quick and cheap to make and change. Speed and low cost suit idea generation.

Exam-style questions

1. Identify two types of model and describe how they could be used in the design process. (4 marks)

Show marking guidance
  • 1 mark per correct type, 1 mark per use
  • Sketch model: quickly generate/explore ideas or check proportions
  • Block model: test ergonomics, show form
  • Scale model: check proportion against users/environment
  • CAD model: test colours/materials, share with team, link to CAM
